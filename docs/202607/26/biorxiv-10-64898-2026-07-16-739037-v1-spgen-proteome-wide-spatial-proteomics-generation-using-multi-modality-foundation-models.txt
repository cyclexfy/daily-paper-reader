Abstract 13 

Spatial proteomics (SP) measures the spatial distribution of proteins within tissues, 14 

providing important insights into tissue function, disease, and therapeutic response. 15 

However, current SP technologies profile only a small fraction of the proteome and are 16 

limited by cost and measurement noise. Recent AI approaches enable predicting spatial 17 

protein expression from transcriptomic or histopathological data, but are typically 18 

restricted to paired datasets covering only tens of proteins, limiting their ability to 19 

generalize beyond experimentally measured protein panels. Here we present SPgen, a 20 

multi-modal foundation-model framework for proteome-wide spatial protein prediction. 21 

SPgen integrates protein sequences, functional annotations, transcriptomic profiles, and 22 

spatial information to learn transferable representations that enable inference beyond 23 

experimentally profiled proteins. Across diverse spatial proteomics datasets, SPgen 24 

accurately reconstructs measured spatial patterns, reduces measurement noise, and 25 

enables proteome-wide spatial prediction. 26 

27 

Introduction 28 

Spatial proteomics (SP) enables direct measurement of protein distribution within 29 

tissues 1 , providing critical insights into development, immunity, tumor biology, and 30 

disease progression, etc 2,3 . Recent imaging- and mass spectrometry–based technologies 31 

have substantially expanded the scale of spatial protein profiling 4-6 . However, current 32 

SP platforms remain constrained by limited protein coverage, high cost 7 , and 33 

measurement noise. Even the largest SP datasets capture only a small fraction of the 34 

proteome, while important protein classes, including transcription factors (TFs) 8 and 35 

membrane proteins 9 , are often underrepresented owing to technical limitations. These 36 

challenges remain major barriers to comprehensive spatial characterization of the 37 

proteome. 38

Here, we developed SPgen, a multi-modal representation learning framework for 48 

proteome-wide spatial protein prediction. SPgen is built on the hypothesis that protein 49 

spatial organization is jointly determined by intrinsic protein properties and cellular 50 

context 15 . To model these complementary factors, SPgen jointly learns protein 51 

representations from protein sequences 16 , functional annotations 17 , and transcriptomic 52 

profiles 18 , and spot representations from SP data. By integrating protein and spot 53 

embeddings, SPgen learns transferable relationships between protein identity and 54 

spatial organization. As a result, SPgen could generate spatial distribution for proteins 55 

lacking direct measurements, extending inference beyond experimentally profiled 56 

panels. Across multiple mass spectrometry–based SP datasets, SPgen accurately 57 

reconstructs measured spatial patterns and generalizes to previously unseen proteins, 58 

including transcription factors and membrane-associated proteins. 59

Results 62 

Overview of SPgen 63 

SPgen aims to infer proteome-wide spatial protein distributions beyond the proteins 64 

measured in a given SP dataset. For this purpose, SPgen jointly learns protein 65 

representations and spatial-context representations to enable spatial prediction for 66 

unmeasured proteins ( Fig. 1 ). 67 

68 

Protein representations are derived from three complementary modalities: protein 69 

annotations, amino acid sequences, and gene-level transcriptomics. Specifically, SPgen 70 

encodes UniProt 17 protein descriptions using a pretrained large language model 71 

(LLM) 19,20 such as PubMedBERT 21 , protein sequences using a protein language model 72 

(for example, ESM 22 ), and gene-level transcriptomics using pretrained single-cell 73 

foundation models 23,24 (for example, scGPT 24 ). These embeddings are integrated into a 74 

unified protein representation that captures functional, biochemical, and genomics 75 

profiles. 76 

77 

To model spatial context, SPgen learns spot representations from measured spatial 78 

proteomic profiles using a Graph Neural Network (GNN) 25,26 that incorporates 79 

neighborhood information. During training, proteins are partitioned into training and 80 

testing sets, and only training proteins are used to construct spot representations, 81 

preventing information leakage. SPgen then predicts spatial abundance by combining 82 

protein and spot representations and is optimized (see Methods for details). Once 83

205

Discussion 277 

Spatial omics is rapidly advancing our ability to resolve tissue organization at molecular 278 

resolution. Recent mass spectrometry–based SP technologies enable the quantification 279 

of thousands of proteins per spatial location, substantially improving proteome 280 

coverage. However, this coverage remains far from complete, and is fundamentally 281 

constrained by the high cost and throughput limitations of MS-based assays. As a result, 282 

there is an inherent trade-off between proteome coverage and spatial resolution, which 283 

limits large-scale, routine profiling and constrains biological discovery in spatial 284 

proteomics. These limitations highlight the need for computational approaches that can 285 

extend effective protein coverage beyond experimentally measurement. 286 

287 

Here we present SPgen, a multi-modal framework for proteome-wide spatial protein 288 

prediction. SPgen integrates protein sequences, functional annotations, and genomics 289 

signals to learn transferable representations across molecular modalities. Across 290 

multiple SP datasets, SPgen accurately reconstructs spatial patterns of unseen proteins, 291 

enabling proteome-scale spatial prediction and supporting downstream analyses. In 292 

addition, SPgen improves signal consistency in measured data by reducing spatial noise, 293 

thereby enhancing the biological interpretability of SP profiles. 294 

295 

Despite these advances, SPgen is currently trained and evaluated on individual SP 296 

datasets, which may limit its generalization across platforms. This is partly due to the 297 

intrinsic noise and sparsity of MS-based spatial proteomics and the limited and 298 

imbalanced coverage of protein measurements used for supervision, which may 299 

introduce dataset-specific biases. 300 

301 

Looking forward, SPgen provides a general framework for scalable spatial proteomics 302 

modeling. Its modular design allows straightforward integration of additional 303 

modalities, such as spatial transcriptomics and imaging-based measurements. With the 304 

continued expansion of spatial multi-omics technologies, SPgen may serve as a 305 

foundation for cross-platform models capable of predicting spatial protein distributions 306 

across tissues and experimental conditions. 307

Learning spot embeddings with GAT 370 

The spot embeddings are learned from spatial proteomics data using a multi-layer GAT. 371 

The spatial graph is constructed based on tissue coordinates using a K-nearest neighbors 372 

strategy (K = 4), ensuring that each spot is connected to its four closest spatial neighbors. 373 

Using the preprocessed protein abundance vectors from the training set as node features, 374 

the GAT model propagates local spatial information through four stacked GATConv 375 

layers, each followed by layer normalization and residual connections to stabilize 376 

training and enhance representation capacity. 377

Evaluation with Pearson correlation 446 

On each SP dataset, after being trained on the training set comprising thousands of 447 

proteins, SPgen is capable of generating spatial distributions for any queried protein on 448 

the corresponding tissue, based on its embedding from the multi-modality foundation 449 

model. To evaluate predictive accuracy, we first inferred spatial maps for proteins in 450 

the testing partition. The Pearson correlation between predicted and experimentally 451 

measured expression patterns is employed as the evaluation metric. The joint 452 

distribution of testing scores and spatial autocorrelation (Moran’s I) enables assessment 453 

of how SPgen performance varies with the underlying spatial structure of individual 454 

proteins. 455

The proteins clustering 458 

To stratify proteins according to their spatial expression patterns, we performed 459 

unsupervised clustering based on their spot-level abundance profiles. We standardized 460 

each protein expression vector to zero mean and unit variance using z-score scaling, 461 

ensuring that differences in magnitude did not dominate downstream analyses. 462 

Principal component analysis (PCA) is then applied to obtain a low-dimensional 463 

representation of protein-level variation. PCA is performed using 50 components and a 464 

deterministic random seed at 42 to ensure reproducibility. The resulting principal 465 

components are subsequently subjected to k-means clustering, grouping proteins into 466 

clusters according to their distribution patterns. 467 

468

spot-level protein expression profiles. We first selected the top 2000 spatially variable 472 

proteins according to the Moran’s I, and perform principal component analysis to retain 473 

the top 50 components to capture the dominant axes of variation across spots. A shared 474 

nearest-neighbor graph is subsequently constructed in the reduced space using 15 475

The ablation study on multi-modality protein embeddings 493 

We perform an ablation analysis to assess the contribution of each protein embedding 494 

modality to the overall model performance. Specifically, we considered three 495 

embedding sources: (i) expression-informed representations with scGPT, (ii) sequence-496 

derived representations from ESM, and (iii) text-informed representations with 497 

pubmedBERT. For each modality, a separate model variant is trained by disabling one 498 

or more embedding inputs while keeping all other components and training 499 

hyperparameters fixed. 500 

501 

The ablations are conducted under identical training and testing sets within each spatial 502 

proteomics dataset. Model performance is evaluated using the same metrics used in the 503 

baseline setting, enabling direct comparison among single-modality, dual-modality, and 504 

full multi-modality configurations. 505
