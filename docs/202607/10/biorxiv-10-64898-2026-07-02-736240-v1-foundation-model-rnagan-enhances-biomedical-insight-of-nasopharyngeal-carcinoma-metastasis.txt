ABSTRACT 21 

RNAGAN (version 2.0 , https://github.com/ZhaozhengHou -HKU/RNAGAN -2.0.git ) is a22 

published foundation model that analyzes single -cell and bulk -level RNA sequencing samples 23 

and enables multiple applications that enhance medical insights. Here we applied this model to 24 

Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC) as in -context few -short format (i.e., the model was never 25 

trained with any NPC data). We conducted all four supported functions, which include sample 26 

stratification, vectorization, pseudo data generation, and marker identification. The results were 27 

then used for identifying metastatic NPC and to investigate mechanisms associated with NPC 28 

metastasis. 29 

30 

Examination with stratification showed that the accuracy of RNAGAN results for evaluating 31 

the metastasis risk in NPC patients are comparable to or outcompeted recently published risk 32 

estimation linear prediction model. Vectorization results present consistency across multiple 33 

cohorts and RNAGAN model versions. In the task of identifying markers and mechanisms 34 

related to NPC metastasis, incorporating pseudo data substantially enhanced the 35 

representativeness of single -cohort -based differential expression (DE) analysis. Moreover, 36 

RNAGAN identified metastasis -related marker genes based on single cohort, were concordant 37 

with the ground truth obtained across multiple cohorts (p=1.05e -9). 38 

39 

Regarding biomedical mechanisms, RNAGAN enabled second -order feature extraction, 40 

unveiling a remarkable domination of the protective function of adaptive immune responses 41 

(as indicated by IL21R levels) over the hazardous function of chronic, non -resolving innate 42 

inflammation ( as indicated by S100A8 levels) against NPC metastasis after first -line treatment. 43 

This association demonstrates a high degree of consistency with the external cohort. 44 

45 

This study demonstrates the utility of the foundation model RNAGAN in uncovering 46 

therapeutic insights for novel cancer types without extra training. We reveal a critical spatial 47 

mechanism preventing distant metastasis via humoral anti -tumor immunity in NPC . High 48 

S100A8 expression by innate antigen -presenting cells (APCs) triggers an inflammatory cascade 49 

promoting epithelial -mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis. However, when germinal 50 

center IL21R + B cells simultaneously colocalize with these innate signals, they override this 51 

suppressive tissue stress. Spatial analysis shows that a high S100A8 /IL21R intersection within 52 

tumor regions strictly distinguishes treatment responders, whereas non -responders display 53 

spatial mismatch or S100A8 + hyper -infiltration. This coordinated innate -adaptive cross -talk 54 

sustains functional tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) that mature IgG -secreting plasma cells, 55 

which opsonize and eliminate emerging EMT tumor cells before systemic escape. 56 

Consequently, while S100A8 alone is an unreliable prognosticator, its spatial colocalization 57 

with IL21R is a robust protective indicator overlooked by conventional bulk analysis methods. 58

Introduction 60 

RNAGAN (available at  https://github.com/ZhaozhengHou -HKU/RNAGAN -1.0.git ) [1] is a 61 

published foundation model for biomedicine research, designed with generate adversarial 62 

network (GAN) structures [2, 3] . This model aims to process RNA sequencing data at single -63 

cell or bulk level and is trained with real human sample s only . The major advantages include 64 

the capability with small datasets (20 to 30 samples), its explicit pathway enrichment analysis 65 

layer and the corresponding feature extraction , and the structural prevention of data 66 

memorization and privacy leakage [1, 4] .67 

68 

Same as other foundation models that have demonstrated remarkable capacity for biomarker 69 

discovery and patient stratification [5] , the clinical and biomedical applications of these models 70 

are hindered by the lack of comprehensive and detailed validation for disease s or cancer types 71 

that fall outside of the training distributions [6, 7] . While common malignancies are often well -72 

represented in large -scale repositories, rare or regional diseases or cancer types lack the 73 

necessary longitudinal data and molecular profiling consistency required to rigorously test 74 

model generalizability [8, 9] . For this consideration, the evaluation with a "Goldilocks" 75 

selection of cancer type for evaluation , sufficiently rare to ensure genuine out -of -distribution 76 

evaluation, yet have widely accepted biomedical insights and enough standardized molecular 77 

depth to permit statistically significant benchmarkin g is of particular value [10] .78 

79 

In this study, we chose Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC) , particularly its metastasis and 80 

response to treatments as the tasks for evaluat ing the performance of RNAGAN. NPC is a 81 

malignancy occurring at nasopharynx , with a high incidence in southern China, Southeast Asia, 82 

and parts of North Africa [11 -13] . The development of clinical oncology in recent decades have 83 

confirmed the importance of tumor microenvironment (TME) to the development, metastasis 84 

and drug resistance [14, 15] . The TME is an aggregation of stromal, immune, tumoral cells, 85 

and extracellular components within tumorous regions . It is also where mechanisms such as 86 

adaptive immune responses [16] and nonspecific inflammation [17] determine cancer 87 

outcomes, and so as the tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) formed to promote apoptosis of 88 

malignant cells and enhance immunotherapy response [18] . Despite the fact that research on 89 

these metastasis -related mechanisms has been conducted for decades, as is the case with other 90 

cancer types, there are still many details that remain unknown , particularly the interaction s and 91 

dominations of these mechanisms. 92 

93 

In this study, we evaluated the benefit of RNAGAN in NPC metastasis -prediction and related 94 

tasks with two bulk RNA sequencing data sets from Hong Kong and Guangzhou [19] , one 95 

single cell data set [19] , and one spatial transcriptomic data set [18] . We conducted all four 96 

supported functions, which include sample stratification, vectorization, pseudo data generation, 97 

and marker identification. Particularly, one of the second order features (association of two 98 

marker genes) related to distant metastasis was e valuated in detail . Moreover, we introduced 99 

two more updated AI reasoning mechanisms to RNAGAN and established enhanced versions 100 

(RNAGAN -Quadra and RNAGAN -GPT). 101

New developments in RNAGAN 2.0 124 

In accordance with the published RNAGAN version 1.0 [1] , two significant modifications have 125 

been implemented with the objective of enhancing the system's robustness and mechanical 126 

support of biomedical features : the replacement of the normalization layer, and the introduction 127 

of two variations of representation shaping layers at the network bottleneck (details can be 128 

found in  Figure S 1).129 

130 

RNAGAN 1.0 adopted batch normalization (normalization of layer outputs across all input 131 

cases within a single batch during network training ), and they are replaced by layer 132 

normalization [24] (normalize the layer outputs for each input case) in the new version . This 133 

replacement is for the consideration that the network should facilitate stable process and 134 

reasoning for each input case, regardless of the condition of other inputs that provided in the 135 

same batch (which could originate from different cohorts in network training, and not available 136 

in application scenarios). As indicated by prior research, this replacement has the potential to 137 

enhance both the precision and the extent to which the results generalize [25, 26] . Notably, this 138 

modification does not encompass the final normalization layer for discriminators. The rationale 139 

behind this choice stems from the fact that the training procedure for discriminators 140 

consistently utilizes balanced positive and negative input cases for each batch, an approach that 141 

has been recommended by numerous studies [27] .142 

143 

For bottleneck representation shaping , we introduced two variations to replace the initial fully -144 

connection layer : The first variation is the quadratic layer (designated as RNAGAN -Quadra in 145 

this paper) [28, 29] , and the second variation is the transformer block [30, 31] (designated as 146 

RNAGAN -GPT). The quadratic layer is designed to compute the squares and product values 147 

of the input for this layer, thereby ensuring that the downstream process can directly base its 148 

operations on the values such as  𝑥 1 

> 2

and  𝑥 1 ∗ 𝑥 2 . This design explicitly supports the 149 

understanding of U/J -shaped non -linearity and interactions (epistasis) between genes and/or 150 

pathways, which are extremely common in biomedical mechanisms [32] . Multiple 151 

authoritative studies and practical experiences have reported the performance improvement of 152 

this design [26, 33] . The transformer block has garnered a more substantial degree of 153 

recognition, and hybrid U-net -like architectures with the transformer serving as the 154 

representation shaping layer have demonstrated their efficacy in numerous recent studies [34, 155 

35] .156

Function 1: Stratification 165 

In this study, we carried out stratification for single -cell (with the task of identifying tumor 166 

cells), and for bulk -sequencing data (with the task of identifying NPC patients that will develop 167 

distance metastasis within 3 years after first -line treatment). Single -cell task was accomplished 168 

in the positive reference only format, because the cells are clustered into 10 types [19] , and 20 169 

randomly selected negative samples may not be representative enough. Bulk -sequencing 170 

validation used both positive (metastatic NPC) reference group and negative (non -metastatic 171 

NPC) group. 172

Function 2: Vectorization of sample populations 182 

Apart from the metrics directly evaluating the stratification accuracy, vectorization results is 183 

also an approach to confirm the representation validity / biological fidelity of RNAGAN. A 184 

foundation model that captures correct and biologically meaningful features, should present 185 

the division of different patient groups (such as metastatic and non -metastatic NPC), and this 186 

pattern should be robust across different cohorts. For both cohort 1 and cohort 2, 100 vectors 187 

were generated for metastatic/non -metastatic NPC populations using the bootstrapping method. 188 

The principal component analysis (PCA) was computed for the purpose of visualization. 189 

190 

The generator sub -network of RNAGAN can generate a 64 -dimentional vector for a given 191 

group of 20 samples as reference. The generated vector is for the given reference group rather 192 

than one single sample, because the variance between references is also incorporated into the 193 

vectorization procedure. 194

Results 247 

We conducted all four functions as stated in the introduction paper of RNAGAN [1] , which 248 

include: sample stratification, marker identification, pseudo data generation, and vectorization. 249 

Subsequently, the results were utilized to investigate underlying mechanisms associated with 250 

NPC metastasis. 251 

252 

RNAGAN can Estimate NPC Metasta sis Tendency with Clinically Usable Accuracy and 253 

Biological Fidelity 254 

In this study, we applied the AI model to identify patients developed distant metastasis within 255 

three years after the first -line treatment (according to the RNA -sequencing results of the 256 

primary tumor biopsy before the first -line treatment) ( Figure 1A-B) It is noteworthy that all of 257 

these results are derived from in -context few -short applications, and the model has not been 258 

trained on any NPC data. 259 

260 

RNAGAN 2.0 (both Quadra and GPT versions) achieved significantly better performance than 261 

RNAGAN 1.0. Particularly, the Quadra versions achieved the accuracy level that is comparable 262 

to or even outcompeted a recently published linear prediction model [19] (derived from typical 263 

clinical medicine research (LASSO) [37, 38] ) in the within cohort test. However, the 264 

performance significantly reduced in the cross -cohort application test (building reference 265 

groups with cohort 1 samples and evaluate metastatic risk for each NPC sample from cohort 266 

2). The versions with predefined pathways (reported gene sets from Human Molecular 267 

Signatures Database as detailed in [1] ) led to higher AUCs compared to the versions without 268 

the pathway layer. However, the versions with learned pathways (same number of pathways 269 

but the weight factors were learned from scratch based on during single -cell data training) did 270 

not present a significant improvement in the accuracy. Therefore, it is reasonable to conclude 271 

that the most recommended version for metastasis risk stratification is RNAGAN -Quadra with 272 

predefined pathways .273 

274 

We generated the vectorization results for metastatic and non -metastatic NPC populations from 275 

the latent spaces of RNAGAN Quadra and GPT versions ( Figure 1C). The vectors demonstrate 276 

a significant separation between NPC patients with different clinical outcomes, and the spatial 277 

relationship in the latent space is consistent across cohorts 1 and 2. Furthermore, despite never 278 

interacting with each other during training, the Quadra and GPT versions exhibit similar spatial 279 

location patterns in PCA. The PC1 for RNAGAN -GPT is flipped to show consistency , which 280 

is reasonable since PCA only considers the axes of principal components (PC), and the positive 281 

direction is random and mathematically equivalent. 282 

283 

We have also evaluated the performance of RNAGAN with few other tasks: single -cell -level 284 

application that identify NPC tumor cells from other cell types, achieving area under the curve 285 

(AUC) scores above 95%; and the stratification of three molecular subtypes [39] , with the AUC 286 

values exceeding 80% for within -cohort applications (Figure S 2).287

RNAGAN Quadra and GPT versions present very similar trends of metrics regarding different 295 

ratios of pseudo data to real data. The sensitivity of DE analysis kept improving by adding 296 

more pseudo data and achieved 95% sensitivity with 1:1 pseudo -real data ratio from less than 297 

60% by real data only. It means that RNAGAN generators captured more than 95% of “real” 298 

marker genes and helped to make these markers more significant with the generated pseudo 299 

data. The p values from fisher exact test for up -regulated markers improved with more pseudo 300 

data, suggesting that these generated data improved the enrichment of real markers in the DE 301 

analysis (the improvement in down -regulated markers is not as significant because the p value 302 

is already very significant from cohort 1 by its own). However, F1 score and precision 303 

decreased since adding more than 25% of pseudo data, suggesting that RNAGAN was also 304 

adding its own features to the data and introduced some false -positive markers. 305 

306 

In conclusion, adding 75 -100% pseudo data helped to reduce type II error s (false negatives that 307 

miss true markers). If users also want to avoid type I error s (false positives that introduce false 308 

markers into the downstream analysis ), adding 25% pseudo data significantly improves marker 309 

filtering. It is important to note that these evaluation metrics (such as Wilcoxon p values) would 310 

not improve by simply repeating the real samples. This indicates that RNAGAN truly makes 311 

novel contributions to the analysis and leads to more robust and representative results. 312

Identified Association between S100A8 and IL21R Validated in Multi -omics Datasets 335 

From the gene -level associations identified by RNAGAN, we chose the domination of IL21R 336 

levels over the hazardous function indicated by S100A8 levels as an example to carry out 337 

further investigation ( Figure 4).338 

339 

As indicated by RNAGAN, IL21R + cases are less likely to develop distant metastasis after 340 

receiving first -line treatment, while high S100A8 expression indicates a higher metastasis risk, 341 

and the effect of IL21R dominates the overall risk level over the mechanism represented by 342 

S100A8 . In other words, S100A8 +IL21R + cases are more likely to respond well to treatment, 343 

while S100A8 +IL21R - cases have a worse response. 344 

345 

Although RNAGAN identified the above association based on cohort 1 data only, this pattern 346 

was also observed in cohort 2 (Figure 4A) and so as spatial transcriptomic dataset in terms of 347 

the respo nse to immunotherapy ( Figure 4B). A detailed view of the representative slides in 348 

Figure 4B illustrates how the spatial organization of innate antigen -presenting cells (APCs) 349 

shapes the adaptive germinal center response. Non -responder 3 entirely lacks overlapping 350 

signals for the two marker genes, suggesting that the recruitment and activation of adaptive, 351 

germinal center IL21R + B cells are not initiated when the baseline stimulus from innate 352 

S100A8 + APCs (such as monocytes, dendritic cells, or macrophages) falls below a critical 353 

threshold. Conversely, responder 1 and 7 (as examples) exhibit robust recruitment of germinal 354 

center IL21R + B cells, demonstrating that even a modest presence of S100A8 + APCs can 355 

successfully drive adaptive humoral immunity. Non -responder 1 , however, highlights a 356 

dysregulation in this cross -talk that S100A8 + APCs widely infiltrate the stromal regions and 357 

dominate the tissue landscape, yet germinal center IL21R + B cells remain scarce and isolated, 358 

indicating that overabundant, hyper -inflammatory innate myeloid cell accumulation correlates 359 

with a failure to sustain the structured microenvironment required for localized germinal center 360 

reactions. 361 

362 

Beyond these broad trends, we observed more nuanced spatial distribution patterns across our 363 

dataset, as demonstrated by the representative architecture of responder 5 (Figure 4C ). Here, 364 

S100A8 expression is highly localized within tumor regions but completely excluded from 365 

tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS). Notably, S100A8 + APC presence extends into stromal cell 366 

regions, predominantly in cases characterized by clinical non -response to treatment. In contrast, 367 

IL21R + germinal center B cells populate all three microenvironmental regions (tumor, stroma, 368 

and TLS) and display pronounced colocalization with S100A8 + cells , particularly within 369 

S100A8 -enriched samples. 370 

371 

To quantify this cellular interplay, we compared the Intersection over Union (IoU) of S100A8 372 

and IL21R signals within tumor regions ( Figure 4D ). This spatial metric revealed that good 373 

responders to therapy exhibit significantly higher IoU values than non -responders. Critically, 374 

100% of responders maintain an IoU above a minimum threshold of 0.01, whereas 80% of non -375 

responders fall below this baseline. Our spatial analysis indicates that low IoU values stem 376 

primarily from two distinct structural pathologies: either adaptive IL21R + cells fail to enrich 377 

within S100A8 + tumor zones, or the tumor regions become almost entirely saturated by 378 

S100A8 + APCs, effectively crowding out adaptive immune responders. 379 

380 

Importantly, while elevated S100A8 expression contextually amplifies the prognostic power of 381 

IL21R , rendering it a more substantial protective marker , S100A8 expression alone is not a 382 

robust predictor of clinical outcome. Consequently, standard bulk or non -spatial analytical 383

Discussions 387 

In this study, we applied the RNAGAN (a published AI foundation model for analyzing single -388 

cell and bulk -level RNA sequencing data) to Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma, a cancer type that 389 

has not been included in the training data set of this tool. All the four suggested analysis 390 

functions have been carried out and validated in the in -context few -shot mode (i.e., directly use 391 

the model to process NPC sample data without model fine -tune): sample stratification, 392 

vectorization , pseudo data generation, and marker identification . In addition, we have selected 393 

one of the mechanisms identified by this model and validated with an independent cohort and 394 

spatial transcriptomic dataset. 395 

396 

Regarding the task of identifying metastatic NPC, RNAGAN has demonstrated the capacity to 397 

distinguish metastatic NPC patients with AUC scores that are comparable to or exceed those 398 

of a recently published linear estimation model. And vectorization of metastatic and non -399 

metastatic NPC presented a high degree of consistency in the latent representations (with PCA) 400 

across both RNAGAN versions and cohorts, suggesting that key underlying structures of the 401 

data have been robustly captured and processed. 402 

403 

In terms of identifying metastasis -related markers and mechanisms, adding pseudo data from 404 

RNAGAN into real data sets, made the downstream differential expression (DE) analysis more 405 

robust. The sensitivity of ground truth gene markers exhibited an increase from less than 60% 406 

to over 95%. The F1 score significantly improved and maximized by adding 25% of pseudo 407 

data. RNAGAN identified 59 marker genes and 98 pathways that contribute to correct 408 

estimation of NPC metastasis risk. It also identified 41 associations between genes, and 37 409 

pathway associations. 410 

411 

A major challenge in contemporary cancer transcriptomics is the reliance on single -marker 412 

prognostic indices, which frequently oversimplify the complex, multicellular dynamics of the 413 

tumor microenvironment (TME). In this study, our foundation model, RNAGAN, bypassed 414 

these classical constraints to uncover a novel, highly coordinated protective mechanism 415 

governed by the spatial interplay between S100A8 and IL21R . Remarkably, while this gene -416 

level association was initially identified within our primary cohort, its distinct distribution 417 

pattern was independently validated across both an external bulk -sequencing cohort and a 418 

spatial transcriptomics dataset. 419 

420 

The clinical significance of this relationship lies entirely within its spatial coordination, a 421 

feature that standard analytical pipelines routinely overlook. When analyzed in isolation, 422 

S100A8 does not emerge as a statistically robust or salient indicator of patient survival, leading 423 

to its frequent dismissal in conventional bioinformatic workflows as irrelevant noise. However, 424 

by evaluating the spatial and contextual dependencies between markers, our AI -driven 425 

approach demonstrated that high S100A8 expression serves as a critical prerequisite that 426 

amplifies the protective value of IL21R . This highlights the indispensable role of advanced 427 

RNA GAN model in discovering non -linear, multi -cellular interactions across independent 428 

cohorts that are otherwise invisible to traditional bulk -sequencing analyses. 429 

430 

At the mechanistic level, this interaction reflects a finely balanced crosstalk between innate 431 

inflammation and adaptive humoral immunity, which dictates whether a tumor undergoes 432

an upstream inflammatory cascade mediated via the TLR4 receptor and the NLRP3 435

Our quantitative spatial analysis further refines this model by demonstrating that the physical 440 

architecture of these cells directly correlates with therapeutic success. As evidenced by our 441 

Intersection over Union (IoU ) metrics, a definitive hallmark of clinical responders is a high 442 

spatial overlap between S100A8 and IL21R signals within the tumor zones. This spatial 443 

proximity ensures that the newly organized TLS can efficiently orchestrate the local maturation 444 

of naïve B cells into IgG -secreting plasma cells. Situated at the invasive tumor border, these 445 

plasma cells immediately opsonize emerging EMT tumor cells, marking them for antibody -446

intravasate and escape into the systemic circulation. 448

Conversely, clinical non -responders primarily present with two spatial pathologies that yield a 450 

low IoU , indicating a complete failure of IL21R + B cells to enrich within S100A8 +451

saturate the tumor stroma. In the latter scenario, the overabundant innate population effectively 453 

crowds out adaptive immune responders, disrupting the microenvironmental formatting 454 

required to sustain TLS architecture. Taken together, these findings prove that S100A8 is not a 455 

monolithic "hazardous" factor; rather, its clinical outcome is entirely contextual. When 456 

spatially synchronized with IL21R + B cells, it acts as the initial innate spark that ignites a 457 

protective, localized humoral response capable of neutralizing metastatic progression. 458 

459 

In summary, this work demonstrates the significant value of the foundation model RNAGAN 460 

in establishing robust medical applications and insights for cancer types it encounters for the 461 

first time without extra training. This study enhances confidence in the implementation of this 462 

model for other cancer types and diseases. 463

Future Prospective 465 

A notable benefit of RNAGAN is its capacity to expeditiously establish the stratification of 466 

samples with designated positive and negative references. This process occurs without 467 

requiring the model to be informed of the standard or the mechanism that differentiates the 468 

reference groups. The foundation model has the capacity to analyze gene and pathway -level 469 

markers, as well as the potential associations between them. Consequently, in the subsequent 470 

phase, we will employ RNAGAN to conduct a more comprehensive investigation of the 471 

markers and mechanisms associated with the various NPC molecular subtypes and clinical 472 

outcomes, including relapse. 473 

474 

As a foundation model , RNGAN has been demonstrated to have structural compatibility with 475 

virtual gene perturbation; further validation with multiple datasets is currently underway to 476 

ascertain the corresponding functionality. 477
