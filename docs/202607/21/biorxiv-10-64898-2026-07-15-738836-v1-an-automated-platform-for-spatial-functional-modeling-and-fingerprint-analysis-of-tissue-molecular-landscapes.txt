Abstract 29 

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) enables high -resolution molecular profiling while preserving tissue 30 

architecture, creating new opportunities to investigate how disease -associated pathways are 31 

organized within tissues. However, existing analytical approaches largely focus on individual 32 

pathways or cell types and do not provide a unified framework for modeling spatially varying 33 

pathway interactions across tissue sections and anatomical planes. Here , we present an 34 

integrative framework , Spatial Fingerprints Analytics (SFinx ), that combines reference -free 35 

deconvolution, pathway activity reconstruction, and Spatial Functional Data Analysis (SFDA) to 36 

map localized pathway activity and pathway –phenotype interactions in complex tissues. 37 

Applying SFinx to 10x Visium ST datasets from murine lupus nephritis, we reconstructed 38 

continuous spatial landscapes of pathway activity and disease -associated phenotypes across 39 

kidney sections. This approach identified anatomically restricted inflammatory domains 40 

characterized by coordinated activation of immune pathways and revealed substantial spatial 41 

heterogeneity in pathway crosstalk across renal compartments. Using generalized additive 42 

models with tensor -product splines , we quantified spatially varying associations between lupus 43 

nephritis and neutrophil activation pathways across tissue sections , uncovering regions with both 44 

positive and negative relationships that would be obscured by conventional bulk analyses. Multi -45 

slice integration further demonstrated reproducible spatial interaction patterns while accounting 46 

for section -specific variability. Together, SFinx transforms mixed -spot transcriptomic 47 

measurements into interpretable spatial pathway landscapes and interaction maps, providing a 48 

general framework for identifying localized disease mechanisms. This approach reveals 49 

previously unrecognized spatial organization of inflammatory signaling in lupus nephritis and 50 

presents a broadly applicable strategy for studying spatially coordinated biological processes in 51 

autoimmune disorders, cancer, and neurodegenerative diseases. 52

1.  Introduction 68 

Recent advances in spatial transcriptomics (ST) have transformed tissue -scale molecular 69 

profiling by enabling transcriptome -wide measurements while preserving spatial context [1] .70 

Platforms such as 10x Visium, Slide -seq, and MERFIS H now allow mapping of molecular 71 

programs across intact tissues at resolutions ranging from multicellular regions to near single -cell 72 

levels, revealing cellular neighborhoods, spatial gradients, and microenvironmental interactions 73 

that were previously inaccessible [1,2] . ST has also enabled detailed characterization of tumor 74 

microenvironments by uncovering complex spatial heterogeneity in molecular programs, 75 

immune cell infiltration, and cell –cell interactions, providing important insights into tumor 76 

progression, metastatic potential, and therapeutic response across multiple cancer types [3] .77 

Despite these advances, extracting biologically meaningful information from ST data remains 78 

challenging. Spatially adjacent locations exhibit intrinsic autocorrelation, measurements often 79 

represent mixtures of cell states, and disease -relevant processes frequently emerge at the level of 80 

pathways and tissue niches rather than individual genes. Existing methods address important 81 

components of ST analysis. SpaGCN identifies spatial domains and spatially variable genes [1] ,82 

Giotto provides a comprehensive analysis environment [4] , and stLearn integrates morphology, 83 

trajectories, and cell –cell interactions [5] . However, these approaches do not directly model 84 

spatially varying pathway –pathway or pathway –phenotype relationships across tissue space. 85 

We previously developed geostatistical approaches for modeling continuous molecular 86 

landscapes and quantifying spatial heterogeneity within tissues [6,7] . Although these methods 87 

revealed biologically meaningful spatial structure beyond gene -level analyses, they were not 88 

designed to characterize pathway activity or interactions between pathways across tissue space. 89 

In parallel, our recent work on longitudinal multi -omic and spatial transcriptomic study of lupus 90 

nephritis has revealed enrichment of inflammatory pathways and spatially organized immune 91 

niches in this condition [8] . However, existing analytical tools are limited in their ability to 92 

systematically characterize how disease -relevant pathways interact across anatomical regions, 93 

particularly when these relationships vary spatially. 94 

In this study, we introduce a statistical framework based on two novel features. First, SFinx 95 

introduces the concept of a spatial fingerprint, a quantitative representation of the local activity 96 

of a molecular signature across tissue space. Indeed, for the given transcriptome data of a tissue 97

2.2.  Spatial Fingerprints ( SFinx )177 

The SFinx pipeline consists of four major components. First, we employ STdeconvolve , a 178 

reference -free approach to deconvolve underlying cell types and their transcriptomic profiles 179 

from multi -cellular spatial transcriptomics data [14] . Second, we reconstruct spot -level gene 180 

expression by computing the matrix product of cell -type proportions and cell -type -specific gene 181 

expression profiles. Third, we apply ordered quantile (ORQ) normalization [15] followed by z -182 

score transformation to ensure comparability across spatial locations and tissue sections. Fourth, 183 

we compute single -sample pathway enrichment scores using singscore algorithm [16,17] , a rank -184 

based metric of gene set enrichment that scores the expression activities of gene sets at a single -185 

sample level, enabling us to quantify pathway activity at each spatial location independent of 186 

other samples. 187 

Following pathway scoring, we apply ordinary kriging interpolation with distance -bounded grids 188 

to generate smooth, continuous pathway activity surfaces [18] . This approach creates prediction 189 

grids that only include points within a specified distance threshold from actual tissue spots, 190 

preventing extrapolation into non -tissue regions. The distance threshold is considered as 1.2 191 

times the average nearest -neighbor distance, ensuring biological relevance of interpolated values 192 

while enabling integration of multiple tissue sections onto common spatial coordinates. 193 

SFinx also implements a suite of binary operators for pathway fingerprint analysis. For two 194 

pathway fingerprints defined by threshold -based activation (| Z| ≥ threshold), we computed: (1) 195 

Union: locations where either pathway is activated; (2) Intersection: locations where both 196 

pathways co -activate, quantified by Jaccard similarity index[15]; (3) Difference: locations with 197 

asymmetric activation; and (4) Maximum: the dominant pathway at each location. 198 

To characterize the sensitivity of spatial fingerprints to threshold selection, we additionally 199 

visualized each pathway fingerprint for one of the slices at three rigour levels including low (| Z|200 

≥ 0.5), medium (| Z| ≥ 1.0), and high (| Z| ≥ 1.5) using two -dimensional kernel density estimates of 201 

retained spots using plasma color scale, enabling direct visual assessment of how activation 202 

threshold affects the spatial extent and localization of each pathway fingerprint. 203

2.3.  Spatial Functional Data Analysis (SFDA) 205 

The core innovation of our pipeline lies in its application of spatial functional data analysis 206 

(SFDA) [19,20] which uses spatially varying coefficients to model the pathway -pathway 207 

relationships. Let us denote the spatially varying response pathway enrichment score for a 208 

particular slice as  𝑌 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2) and the predictor pathway score as  𝑋 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2) at longitude  𝑠 1, and 209 

latitude  𝑠 2. We use the following spatially varying function -on -function regression model [19] to 210 

quantify the spatially varying association between the response and predictor pathway scores: 211 

𝑌 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2) = 𝛽 0(𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) + 𝑋 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2)𝛽 1(𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) + 𝜖 (𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) (1). 212 

𝑌 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2) = 𝛽 0(𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) + 𝑋 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2)𝛽 1(𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) + 𝑏 𝑠𝑙𝑖𝑐𝑒 ,0 + 𝑋 (𝑠 1,𝑠 2)𝑏 𝑠𝑙𝑖𝑐𝑒 ,1 + 𝜖 (𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) (2).213 

In the above models  𝛽 1(𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) is a two -dimensional functional slope which captures the spatially 214 

varying associating between the response and the predictor pathway enrichment score,  𝛽 0(𝑠 1, 𝑠 2)215 

is a functional intercept, and  𝜖 (𝑠 1, 𝑠 2) is a mean zero measurement error. The slice -specific 216 

random intercept and slope is denoted by  𝑏 𝑠𝑙𝑖𝑐𝑒 ,0 and  𝑏 𝑠𝑙𝑖𝑐𝑒 ,1. The above models can be seen as 217 

extension of Generalized additive models (GAMs) [21,22] , which are flexible semiparametric 218 

methods that allow the inclusion of both fixed and spatially varying coefficients. The spatially 219 

varying coefficients are modelled using tensor product splines and the coefficients can be 220 

estimated using a penalized least square approach, with explicit smoothness penalty which 221 

allows the coefficients to vary smoothly over space [19,23] , through smoothing functions such as 222 

splines [24] . The above model can be efficiently implemented using the bam function within the 223 

mgcv package [25] in R, where the response pathway expression is modeled as a function of 224 

spatial coordinates (longitude and latitude) and their interaction with predictor pathway 225 

expression. We used 20 cubic B -spline basis functions in both spatial dimensions and chose the 226 

smoothing parameters using the REML method [25] .227

4.  Discussion 397 

Spatial transcriptomics has transformed our ability to study molecular processes within intact 398 

tissues, yet most analytical frameworks remain focused on identifying spatial domains, cell 399 

types, or individual genes [1] . Less attention has been given to modeling how biological 400 

pathways interact across tissue space, despite the fact that disease processes often emerge from 401 

coordinated activity among multiple pathways rather than isolated molecular events. Here, we 402 

present SFinx and SFDA, an integrated framework that reconstructs continuous pathway activity 403 

landscapes and quantifies spatially varying pathway -pathway and pathway -phenotype 404 

relationships. Applying this framework to lupus nephritis revealed anatomically localized 405 

inflammatory niches, compartment -specific pathway interactions, and substantial regional 406 

heterogeneity in neutrophil -associated inflammatory signaling that would be difficult to detect 407 

using conventional analyses .408 

Application of SFinx to lupus nephritis highlighted the complex spatial organization of 409 

inflammatory signaling within diseased kidneys. Although neutrophil activation and lupus 410 

nephritis pathways exhibited areas of overlap, substantial portions of their activity remained 411 

spatially segregated, suggesting that inflammatory injury is organized into distinct molecular 412 

niches rather than a single homogeneous process. Spatial coefficient surfaces further 413 

demonstrated that associations between neutrophil activation and disease -related pathways vary 414 

considerably across tissue compartments, with both positive and negative relationships observed 415 

within the same section. These findings illustrate how spatially varying pathway interactions 416 

may contribute to localized tissue damage and underscore the importance of modeling disease 417 

mechanisms within their anatomical context. 418

A key technical advantage of this design is that aggregating genes into pathways before 419 

modeling should improve statistical stability compared to thousands of individual gene -wise 420 

tests. This is because rank -based gene -set scoring is inherently less sensitive to differences in 421 

sample composition, and penalized smoothers distribute information across neighboring tissue 422 

locations rather than treating each spot in isolation. These are principled expectations grounded 423 

in the properties of the underlying methods, not claims that have been formally benchmarked in 424 

the present study. A second advantage is that converting discrete spot measurements into 425 

continuous spatial surfaces makes cross -compartment and cross -section comparisons more 426 

tractable. Kriging achieves this in a principled way, returning both predicted values and 427 

associated uncertainty at every location rather than simply smoothing for visual appeal [30] .428 

Third, and perhaps most importantly for translational applications, the outputs are inherently 429 

interpretable in biological terms. Pathway activity maps align directly with tissue architecture, 430 

overlap operators quantify whether two pathways occupy the same or distinct spatial domains, 431 

and spatially varying coefficient surfaces reveal not only the strength of association between two 432 

pathways but also where across the tissue that relationship shifts from positive to negative. This 433 

spatial resolution of pathway interactions is especially meaningful in disease, where the same 434 

signaling program can be adaptive in one compartment and pathogenic in another. The 435 

framework is broadly applicable to diseases characterized by spatially organized pathology. 436 

Potential applications include tumor microenvironment analysis in cancer, compartment -specific 437 

inflammation in autoimmune diseases, and lesion -centered molecular organization in 438 

neurodegenerative disorders [31 –35] 439 

Acknowledging the assumptions and limitations inherent in these analytical approaches is 440 

crucial. Deconvolution fundamentally remains an inverse problem that can become 441 

mathematically unstable when cell states are highly similar or when the assay is too sparse to 442 

support reliable de -mixing [14] . Regarding spatial modeling, while Kriging enhances spatial 443 

continuity for visualization, it cannot generate true biological resolution beyond the platform's 444 

physical limits. It also risks over -smoothing sharp compartmental boundaries if the variogram is 445 

mis -specified or if inherently discontinuous biology is modeled as a continuous surface. 446 

Similarly, Spatial Functional Data Analysis (SFDA) assumes that pathway effects vary smoothly 447 

across space. Consequently, the resulting confidence intervals should be interpreted as pointwise 448 

estimates rather than family -wise guarantees across the entire tissue surface [25,26] . Most 449

4.1.  Future Direction s456 

Future work will focus on extending SFinx beyond transcriptomic pathway mapping toward 457 

multimodal spatial inference. One promising direction is the integration of histopathology 458 

images with pathway activity surfaces, enabling prediction of molecular landscapes directly from 459 

routine tissue sections. In parallel, large language models (LLMs) and retrieval -augmented 460 

systems may facilitate interpretation of complex spatial pathway relationships by generating 461 

biologically grounded summaries and linking spatial findings to external biomedical knowledge. 462 

Together, these developments may help bridge spatial omics, computational pathology, and 463 

clinical decision support [31 –33] .464

5.  Conclusion s466 

SFinx and SFDA provide a unified framework for transforming mixed -spot spatial 467 

transcriptomic measurements into continuous pathway landscapes and quantitative maps of 468 

spatial pathway interactions. By moving beyond spatially variable genes and tissue domains 469 

toward direct modeling of pathway -pathway and pathway -phenotype relationships, this approach 470 

enables a more mechanistic understanding of spatially organized disease processes. Application 471 

to lupus nephritis revealed anatomically localized inflammatory niches and compartment -472 

specific pathway crosstalk, illustrating the biological insights that can emerge from pathway -473 

centric spatial analysis. We anticipate that this framework will be broadly applicable across 474 

autoimmune diseases, cancer, neurodegeneration, and other conditions in which disease 475 

mechanisms are spatially heterogeneous and tissue -context dependent. 476
