# ABSTRACT 

Background & Problem: Thyroid Fine-Needle Aspiration Biopsy (FNAB) cytology based on the Bethesda System plays a pivotal role in early thyroid cancer detection; however, deep learning approaches face substantial challenges regarding high false-negative rates and overconfidence under clinical domain shift. 

Methods: In this study, we propose TAM-Chain , a multi-scale ( 10×, 20×, 40× ) thyroid cytology classification framework leveraging Absorbing Markov Chain theory combined with Shannon Entropy-based Uncertainty Quantification. The framework dynamically models multi-magnification feature extraction as an absorbing stochastic process, enabling optimal stopping criteria and a human-in-the-loop referral mechanism to strictly suppress critical diagnostic errors. 

Results: Extensive evaluation on an internal test set ( 𝑁 = 235 ) demonstrates a Macro F1 score of 0.9741 with an absolute False-Negative Rate (FNR) of 0.00% . On an independent external vali-dation set ( 𝑁 = 1015 ) presenting severe domain shift, TAM-Chain maintains superior stability and classification performance (Macro F1 = 0.7026 ) by adaptively adjusting the expected stopping step and triggering specialist referrals, significantly outperforming single-magnification baselines. 

Conclusion: The TAM-Chain framework proves to be a highly effective, safe, and adaptable solu-tion for digital pathology workflows, successfully harmonizing automated diagnostic efficiency with stringent biological safety. 

Keywords Absorbing Markov Chains, Shannon Entropy, Thyroid Cytology, Optimal Stopping, Uncertainty Quantification, Domain Shift Adaptation 

# 1 Introduction 

1.1 Clinical Background and Challenges in Thyroid Cytology Diagnosis 

Fine-Needle Aspiration Biopsy (FNAB) combined with The Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC) serves as the gold standard for clinical evaluation and management of thyroid nodules (Negrelli et al. 2025; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). In clinical practice, three primary risk tiers dictate therapeutic strategies: • Bethesda II (B2 - Benign): Indicates a benign condition manageable via routine clinical follow-up without surgical intervention. • Bethesda V (B5 - Suspicious for Malignancy): Warrants supplementary diagnostic testing or consideration of surgical resection.

• Bethesda VI (B6 - Malignant): Mandates surgical intervention (Poyrazer and Erten 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). The paramount challenge for Medical Artificial Intelligence (Medical AI) systems in this domain lies in controlling the False Negative Rate (FNR) (Catak and Şahinbaş 2022). Misclassifying a patient with malignant cellular features ( 𝐵5 or 𝐵6 ) as benign ( 𝐵2 ) defers critical surgical intervention, incurring catastrophic consequences for patient health and clinical outcomes (Catak and Şahinbaş 2022; Negrelli et al. 2025). Consequently, ensuring clinical safety with FNR ≈ 0% via trustworthy oversight mechanisms represents an indispensable prerequisite for deploying automated diagnostic models into clinical workflows (Rashidisabet et al. 2025; Patel et al. 2026). 

Á Stringent Clinical Safety Requirements In thyroid cancer screening, diagnostic misclassifications from 𝐵5/𝐵6 → 𝐵2 (False Negatives) are strictly unacceptable within clinical protocols. AI models must not only achieve high overall accuracy but also possess reliable referral/deferral mechanisms to delegate high-risk or ambiguous cases to expert pathologists. 

1.2 Limitations of Single-Scale Deep Learning Models and Domain Shift 

Although Deep Learning has driven landmark advancements in medical image analysis, deploying con-ventional machine learning models for multi-scale cytological diagnosis remains constrained by two major technical bottlenecks: • Multi-Scale Computational and Contextual Trade-Off: Analyzing Whole Slide Images (WSIs) requires examination across multiple magnifications (Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). Inspec-tion at low magnification ( 10× ) provides broad spatial architectural context but risks overlooking subtle nuclear cytological details; conversely, exhaustively processing images at high magnification (40× ) incurs substantial computational overhead and memory burdens, while elevating the risk of cellular context loss (Rui et al. 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). • Model Overconfidence Under Domain Shift: When deployed on external validation datasets characterized by distributional shifts—stemming from inter-site variations in staining techniques, scanner models, or slide preparation protocols—deep neural networks frequently exhibit perfor-mance degradation and issue egregious diagnostic errors with undesirably high confidence (Over-confident Errors) (Araújo et al. 2023; Rashidisabet et al. 2025; Hossain et al. 2026).

# 2 Related Work 

2.1 Thyroid Cytology Diagnosis and Limitations of Single-Scale Deep Learning 

Fine-Needle Aspiration Biopsy (FNAB) plays a pivotal role in thyroid cancer screening protocols governed by The Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (Negrelli et al. 2025; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). The application of Deep Learning (DL) models to automated thyroid cytology classification has achieved substantial progress in recent years (Poyrazer and Erten 2026). Early studies primarily focused on deploying Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), such as ResNet or EfficientNet, on image patches extracted at a fixed single magnification level (Poyrazer and Erten 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). However, single-scale approaches exhibit severe constraints in clinical practice: • Low-Magnification Inspection ( 10× ): Models preserve a global architectural overview of cell cluster density and spatial distribution but lack critical nuclear micro-features (e.g., nuclear grooves,  

> 4PREPRINT 2026-09-23

intranuclear inclusions, or psammoma bodies). • High-Magnification Inspection ( 40× ): Models capture detailed nuclear morphological charac-teristics but suffer from cellular context loss, while concurrently escalating computational memory overhead when processing Whole Slide Images (WSIs) (Rui et al. 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). 

2.2 Uncertainty Quantification and Human-in-the-Loop Safeguards 

In safety-critical Medical AI applications, issuing accurate predictions alone is insufficient; models must possess “awareness of their own ignorance” (Uncertainty Awareness) (Kang et al. 2021; Huang et al. 2024). Diagnostic uncertainty in medical imaging is broadly categorized into two forms: • Aleatoric Uncertainty: Arises from intrinsic data noise, non-uniform staining, or out-of-focus and overlapping cellular artifacts. • Epistemic Uncertainty: Stems from model ignorance when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) data absent from the training set. To quantify uncertainty, Shannon Entropy 𝐻(𝑝) computed over the output probability distribution is widely adopted due to its mathematical rigor and minimal computational overhead (Kang et al. 2021; Rui et al. 2026). When integrated into a Human-in-the-Loop Referral mechanism, predictions exhibiting uncertainty above a predefined safety threshold are flagged and deferred to expert pathologists for manual review (Catak and Şahinbaş 2022; Aktas et al. 2026). This strategy effectively suppresses the False Negative Rate (FNR), safeguarding patients against misdiagnosed malignancies (Catak and Şahinbaş 2022; Rashidisabet et al. 2025). 

2.3 Domain Shift and the Challenge of Model Overconfidence 

A major bottleneck hindering the clinical deployment of Medical AI is performance degradation on external validation cohorts—a phenomenon known as Domain Shift (Araújo et al. 2023; Rashidisabet et al. 2025). Cross-institutional variations in WSI scanner hardware, slide preparation protocols, section thickness, and Giemsa/Papanicolaou staining techniques induce significant feature distribution shifts (Negrelli et al. 2025; Hossain et al. 2026). Under domain shift, conventional deep neural networks frequently suffer from Model Overconfidence :outputting erroneous diagnostic predictions with undesirably high confidence scores ( > 95% ) (Rashidis-abet et al. 2025; Hossain et al. 2026). Consequently, naive confidence-gating mechanisms based on maximum softmax probability fail to detect hazardous diagnostic errors. 

2.4 Absorbing Markov Chains in Multi-Tiered Decision Optimization 

The theory of Absorbing Markov Chains (AMCs) provides a mathematically rigorous formulation for modeling stochastic processes featuring terminal absorbing states (Saul I. Gass 2013). In operations research and decision optimization, AMCs facilitate the exact analytical computation of expected obser-vation steps 𝔼[𝜏] and absorption probabilities via the Fundamental Matrix 𝑁 = (𝐼 − 𝑄) −1 (Saul I. Gass 2013). Although Markovian models have been applied to treatment planning and disease progression forecasting (Saul I. Gass 2013), coupling Absorbing Markov Chains with Shannon Entropy as dynamic state-transition triggers across microscopic optical magnifications ( 10× → 20× → 40× ) represents a novel research paradigm.

# 3 Methodology 

3.1 Architectural Overview of the TAM-Chain Framework 

The TAM-Chain (Thyroid Absorbing Markov Chain ) framework is designed as a multi-tier stochastic diagnostic pipeline that integrates Deep Learning for cellular morphological feature extraction with Absorbing Markov Chains for dynamic early stopping and safe referral. Given a Fine-Needle Aspiration Biopsy (FNAB) cytological image sample, the system processes data sequentially across ascending magnification levels: 10× → 20× → 40× . At each magnification tier 𝑡 ∈ {1, 2, 3} , a deep neural network extracts feature vectors and outputs a predicted probability distribution 

𝑝 𝑡 = [𝑝 (𝐵2) 𝑡 , 𝑝 (𝐵5) 𝑡 , 𝑝 (𝐵6) 𝑡 ]𝑇 corresponding to the three representative Bethesda categories. 

3.2 Backbone Architecture and Model Deployment ( ThyroidEffi )

To ensure precise cellular morphological feature extraction across varied magnification levels, TAM-Chain utilizes a specialized backbone model designated as ThyroidEffi 1.0 Base .

3.2.1 Backbone Architecture and Training 

• Architectural Foundation: Built upon optimizations of the high-performance EfficientNet fam-ily, integrating Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks to focus on critical micro-architectural regions of thyroid cell nuclei. • Loss Function and Optimization: The network is trained on the training set using Weighted Cross-Entropy Loss to address class imbalance across Bethesda groups ( 𝐵2, 𝐵5, 𝐵6 ). Optimization is conducted via AdamW with an initial learning rate of 10 −4 and a Cosine Annealing decay schedule. • Data Augmentation: Detailed data augmentation protocols follow the methodologies established in (Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). 

3.2.2 Real-Time Deployment via TorchScript 

• To seamlessly integrate with the Absorbing Markov Chain framework and optimize real-time infer-ence speed, the trained backbone weights are compiled and exported using TorchScript .• Executing inference via TorchScript eliminates Python interpreter overhead, enabling rapid extrac-tion of probability distributions 𝑝 𝑡 at each transition step 10× → 20× → 40× prior to evaluation by the Shannon Entropy function 𝐻(𝑝 𝑡 ).

3.3 Multi-Scale Patch Extraction and Preprocessing 

To provide standardized inputs for the TAM-Chain framework across the three magnification tiers (10×, 20×, 40× ), Whole Slide Image (WSI) preprocessing is executed according to the following protocol: 

3.3.1 Specimen Normalization and Region of Interest (ROI) Segmentation 

• FNAB slides acquired from digital scanners are partitioned into non-overlapping or controlled-overlapping patch grids at base resolution. • To mitigate contextual information loss during localized patch cropping and prevent nuclear detail degradation caused by resizing, the system constructs multi-scale image pyramids corresponding to three objective lens magnifications: 

– 10× Tier ( 𝑆 1): Provides a wide field-of-view (FOV), preserving cellular cluster density, spatial arrangement, and overall thyroid papillary architecture. 

– 20× Tier ( 𝑆 2): Balances local context with intermediate resolution, facilitating clearer iden-tification of epithelial cell clusters. 

– 40× Tier ( 𝑆 3): Zoom-in to microscopic resolution, extracting fine nuclear details (such as psammoma bodies, nuclear grooves, nuclear membrane irregularities, and pale chromatin)— crucial determinants for Bethesda 𝐵5/𝐵6 classification. 

3.3.2 Multi-Scale Feature Alignment 

• Corresponding patch regions sharing identical spatial center coordinates on the WSI are extracted simultaneously across all three magnification tiers. • Each patch at every level is passed through the feature extraction backbone ( ThyroidEffi 1.0 Base ) to generate predicted probability vectors 𝑝 10× , 𝑝 20× , 𝑝 40× , serving as inputs for Shannon Entropy 𝐻(𝑝 𝑡 ) computation and state control within the Absorbing Markov Chain.

6.2 Deciphering Stochastic Domain Shift Adaptation via Markovian Flows 

Under severe Domain Shift on the External Validation cohort, empirical results in Table 3, alongside transition flows in Figure 3 and Figure 4, unveil the unique stochastic self-adaptation capabilities of Absorbing Markov Chains: • Performance Degradation in Single-Scale Baselines (Table 3): The single-scale baseline 

Single 40x experiences the most precipitous drop, declining from 0.9065 internally to 0.6420 

externally (a 26.4% absolute decrease). This reflects the acute vulnerability of high-magnification nuclear features to inter-site staining variations and optical scanning artifacts (Rui et al. 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). • State Distribution Re-allocation (Figure 3): Transitioning to the external domain elevates feature noise, driving up model prediction uncertainty. Consequently, the proportion of cases reaching safe early absorption at 10× drops sharply from 47.7% to 22.3% .• Observation Chain Trajectory Extension (Figure 4): The Markov process adaptively com-pensates by shifting over 71% of cases to the highest optical magnification tier ( 40× ), expanding the expected observation trajectory 𝔼[𝜏] from 2.02 to 2.49 steps (Table 3). • Safety Trigger Activation (Table 3): Specialist referral rates 𝑃 (𝐴 2) automatically expand from 44.65% to 66.84% (Hossain et al. 2026). These empirical metrics in Table 3 and associated visualizations confirm that Absorbing Markov Chains serve not only as computational efficiency optimizers, but also function as an intelligent diagnostic triage system : when input image fidelity degrades due to domain shifts, the framework gracefully defers high-risk decisions to human expertise, systematically resolving the model overconfidence flaw inherent to deep neural networks (Araújo et al. 2023; Rashidisabet et al. 2025).

The marked performance degradation of the 40× baseline on the external cohort (Macro F1 = 0.6420 ,as detailed in Table 3 and Figure 5) underscores that zooming exclusively into nuclear micro-structures risks losing global contextual architecture and increases susceptibility to slide preparation artifacts (Rui et al. 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). 6.4 Limitations and Potential Remedies 

Despite these advancements, several limitations warrant consideration for future iterations: • Scope of Diagnostic Classes: Current evaluations focus on three core diagnostic categories (𝐵2, 𝐵5, 𝐵6 ). Indeterminate categories within the Bethesda system (e.g., Bethesda III - AUS/FLUS and Bethesda IV - FN/SFN) exhibit high intrinsic morphological ambiguity, necessitating an ex-panded Markovian state space (Poyrazer and Erten 2026; Pham Ngoc et al. 2026). • Automation Trade-Offs: Under severe domain shift, the elevation of human referral rates 𝑃 (𝐴 2)

to 66.84% (Table 3) reduces overall AI autonomy. Future work should integrate Domain Adapta-tion techniques to improve the backbone network’s intrinsic confidence under distributional shifts (Araújo et al. 2023; Hossain et al. 2026). 

# 7 Conclusion and Future Work 

7.1 Conclusion 

This study introduced TAM-Chain (Thyroid Absorbing Markov Chain), a novel multi-scale thyroid cytology classification framework combining Absorbing Markov Chains with Shannon Entropy-based Uncertainty Quantification .Through extensive empirical evaluations across 1, 804 internal patients (108 Military Central Hospital) and 1, 015 external patients (Hung Viet Oncology Hospital), our key findings demonstrate: • Absolute Clinical Safety: TAM-Chain paired with the AvgProbs aggregation strategy strictly enforces an absolute False Negative Rate of FNR = 0.00% within the automated AI decision cohort ( 𝐴 1) on internal testing, preventing misclassifications of malignant 𝐵5/𝐵6 tumors. • Adaptive Resilience Under Domain Shift: Faced with external variations in staining pro-tocols and digitizing scanners, the Markov chain adaptively adjusts its stochastic transition be-havior: extending expected observation steps from 𝔼[𝜏] = 2.02 → 2.49 and expanding expert pathologist referral rates 𝑃 (𝐴 2) from 44.65% → 66.84% , establishing a reliable safeguard against out-of-distribution noise. • Computational Efficiency and Variance Stability: Dynamic stochastic early stopping sig-nificantly reduces image processing time compared to fixed high-magnification workflows. Anal-ysis across 1, 000 Bootstrap iterations confirms that AvgProbs preserves minimal variance ( 𝜎 2 =0.000110 ), with statistically significant performance gains confirmed via Friedman testing ( 𝑝 < 0.001 ).
