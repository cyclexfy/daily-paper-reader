# Abstract 23 

Despite the fundamental role of spatial context in driving tumor progression, most 24 

current computational models for virtual perturbation have largely overlooked its 25 

importance. Here, we introduce TMEformer, a tumor microenvironment -aware deep 26 

learning framework that leverages high -resolution spatial transcriptomics to jointly 27 

model intrinsic tumor cell programs and local microenvironmental signals by explicitly 28 

incorporating spatial architecture. Validated across diverse tumor spatial 29 

transcriptomic cohorts, TMEformer enables virtual perturbations that capture functional 30 

dependencies within local cellular ecosystems. Despite being trained on cancer -31 

specific spatial datasets, TMEformer outperforms baseline models pretrained on large -32 

scale corpora in capturing key tumor transitions, including lineage plasticity and the 33 

emergence of therapy resistance. Systematic perturbation analyses prioritize tumor -34 

intrinsic transcription factors and TME -derived ligands that drive disease progression, 35 

recovering established regulators and revealing novel candidates. Furthermore, TME -36 

derived embeddings improve the spatial stratification of tumor cells and align more 37 

closely with pathological architecture. Together, TMEformer establishes a general 38 

framework for modeling tumors as spatially coupled, perturbable ecosystems. 39

# Introduction 42 

With the advent of single -cell and spatial omics, tumor development and progression 43 

are increasingly recognized as emergent properties of complex cellular ecosystems 44 

rather than the consequence of tumor cell –intrinsic alterations alone 1, 2 . Intratumoral 45 

heterogeneity, lineage plasticity, and therapy resistance reflect the capacity of 46 

malignant cells to dynamically adapt to diverse selective pressures 3. These adaptive 47 

processes cannot be fully understood without considering the spatially organized 48 

tumor microenvironment (TME), particularly in solid tumors, which provides 49 

biochemical signals, physical constraints, and intercellular interactions that 50 

continuously shape tumor cell states 4. However, analyses for the tumor ecosystem 51 

defined by tumor cells and surrounding stromal and immune compartments remain 52 

largely descriptive, emphasizing co -localization patterns or static interactions. A central 53 

unresolved challenge remains in deciphering how perturbations in tumor -intrinsic or 54 

microenvironmental compartments propagate through the spatial network to 55 

orchestrate tumor cell states 5, 6 . Addressing this challenge requires computational 56

Concurrently, transformer -based foundation models have revolutionized single -59 

cell representation learning 7, 8 . By pretraining on large -scale single -cell omics data, 60 

these models extract generalizable gene regulatory patterns, facilitating downstream 61 

tasks such as cell type annotation and batch correction. This paradigm has given rise 62 

to the concept of the artificial intelligence virtual cell (AIVC) 9. Leveraging this, in silico 63 

perturbations (ISP) enable the  simulation of genetic or pharmacological interventions 64 

to predict cellular responses, ultimately offering a scalable route to hypothesis 65 

generation and mechanistic inference 10 . However, most single -cell models treat cells 66 

as isolated entities and often rely on  in vitro  data input  11 , which may not fully 67 

recapitulate the  in vivo  responses shaped by the complex tumor microenvironment 68 

(TME)  12 -14 . As spatial transcriptomics (ST) continues to advance, 5 pioneering works 69 

like MintFlow  15  and SpaceTravLR  16  have begun to bridge this gap. Nevertheless, 70 

considerable untapped potential remains in harnessing non -linear deep learning 71 

architectures to capture the intricate, non -canonical regulatory logic underlying cellular 72 

organization. 73 

Here, we introduce TMEformer, a tumor microenvironment –aware spatial 74 

foundation framework. Instead of modeling tumor cells in isolation, TMEformer 75 

explicitly incorporates spatial information from high -resolution ST data to jointly learn 76 

the interactions of tumor -intrinsic transcriptional programs and surrounding 77 

microenvironmental context. For each tumor cell, neighboring cells (conceptually like 78 

dynamic spatial niche) are aggregated through attention -based mechanisms to 79 

generate a compact context token summarizing the local TME, which is subsequently 80 

integrated with the embedding representation of corresponding tumor cell and then 81 

processed by a shared transformer encoder. Based on this design, versatileISP 82 

strategies and tasks are implemented to discover both tumor -intrinsic and TME -83 

derived dependencies, enabling cellular ecosystem -aware causal inference. Moreover, 84 

the learned TME representations yield interpretable embeddings that capture local 85 

tissue architecture and support downstream stratification analysis. Together, 86 

TMEformer establishes a generalizable framework for modeling tumors as spatially 87 

coupled, perturbable ecosystems and provides a foundation for mechanistic 88 

investigation of tumor evolution. 89

# Results 93 

1. A TME -aware Spatial Transformer Model for High Resolution Gene Expression 94 

Prediction 95 

To model the interplay between tumor cells and their neighboring microenvironment, 96 

we introduce a tumor microenvironment -aware single -cell transformer modeling 97 

framework  and train it on high -resolution spatial transcriptomic data (Fig. 1a ). 98 

Specifically, a TME -CEM (TME Context Encoding Module) is designed to enable 99 

integration of each tumor cell’s intrinsic transcriptional profile with signals from its 100 

immediate spatial microenvironment. For a given tumor cell, the module first 101 

represents the initial embeddings of its spatially neighboring TME cells encoded by 102 

overall transcriptome and annotated cell types. These TME embeddings are 103 

processed through two sequential attention layers to model intercellular 104 

communications and aggregated into a single context token, which is then fused with 105 

the tumor cell’s intrinsic embedding using a fixed weight ( Methods ). Thus, our  TME -106 

aware spatial Trans former  (TMEformer) encoder infers context -dependent regulatory 107 

drivers of tumor heterogeneity, enabling a systems -level view of the tumor ecosystem. 108 

It achieves this by integrating spatial architecture to first model the distinct cellular 109 

components surrounding each tumor cell and then orchestrate their complex interplay, 110 

thereby advancing far beyond conventional single cell gene regulatory network (GRN) 111 

inference. 112 

For TMEformer to achieve optimal performance, high resolution spatial 113 

transcriptomic data is essential. For this reason, we acquired clinical spatial 114 

transcriptomic data on eight prostate cancer (PCa) specimens spanning key disease 115 

stages using the Xenium -5K platform ( Figs. S1a -b). This platform offers unparalleled 116 

subcellular resolution for a panel of 5,000 predefined genes, which we extended by 117 

adding customized probes for 51 PCa associated targets ( Supplementary Table 1 ). 118 

After standard processing and quality control, we obtained spatial profiles for 5051 119 

genes across 1,495,698 cells ( Methods ). Major cell types were annotated using 120 

canonical markers, identifying seven main populations: epithelial cells (PCa cells, 121 

49.2%), fibroblasts (24.2%), T cells (9.6%), endothelial cells (7.0%), myeloid cells 122 

(6.8%), B cells (2.5%), and mast cells (0.7%) ( Figs. S1c -f). Subsequently, we 123 

performed both tumor -specific and TME -aware continual training on this dataset, 124 

enabling the model to adapt to the specific transcriptional landscape of prostate tumors 125 

while integrating spatial microenvironmental context. In sample -independent 126

2. TMEformer Demonstrates Robust Performance in Predicting Cellular 132 

Response under In Silico Perturbation 133 

Cellular responses to signals are shaped by complex microenvironmental interactions 134 

that most existing single -cell foundation models fail to capture. By explicitly 135 

incorporating spatial context, TMEformer provides a more realistic modelling 136 

framework. We next assessed its performance in predicting such responses from 137 

different perspectives by various in silico perturbation (ISP) experiments ( Fig. 1b ,138 

Methods ). Three baseline models were used for benchmarking: the original 139 

Geneformer model (GF_PR), its continued -training counterpart on pan -cancer scRNA -140 

seq data (GF_CL), and a version further trained on our PCa spatial transcriptomic data 141 

without incorporating spatial TME information (GF_PCa). 142 

Changes in marker gene expression provide an interpretable readout of how 143 

ISPs influence cellular phenotypes. To this end, we designed a fine -tuning task to 144 

predict gene expression under stimulated perturbations ( Fig. 2a , Methods ). Focusing 145 

on neuroendocrine prostate cancer (NEPC)  – a therapy -resistant state arising from 146 

adenocarcinoma through profound molecular reprogramming  – we accessed the 147 

model’s capability to recapitulate this process, using a panel of canonical NEPC 148 

markers (SYP, CHGA, ENO2, and NCAM1) as the molecular readout. After model fine -149 

tuning, we validated its predictions across multiple ISP tasks using established 150 

drivers of neuroendocrine differentiation: overexpression (OE) of SOX2 17  and 151 

ASCL1 18 , combined loss of PTEN, RB1, and TP53 (TKO)  19 , and LIF -LIFR, the top 152 

ligand -receptor (L -R) pair for lineage plasticity identified in a recent study 20 . Target 153 

gene(s) were shifted to the highest or lowest expression rank to simulate 154 

overexpression or knockdown (KD), respectively, with the resulting effect quantified by 155 

changes in the endpoint cell embedding ( Methods ). TMEformer showed significant 156 

upregulation of NEPC markers following SOX2 or ASCL1 overexpression, as well as 157 

TKO perturbation, compared with random perturbations ( Figs. 2b ). Notably, it also 158 

exhibits the most robust performance, achieving the best results in three of the four 159 

tasks ( Figs. 2c, S2 ). Moreover, among all the 15 candidate L –R pairs identified in the 160 

previous study 20 , TMEformer successfully predicted over 60% of pairs with significant 161

3. Screening of Intrinsic Cell Fate Determinants During Tumor Cell Evolution 198 

Having validated TMEformer’s predictive accuracy, we next leveraged its unique 199 

capability to perform large -scale in silico perturbation screening under spatially 200 

resolved tumor microenvironmental contexts. To systematically identify intrinsic 201 

transcriptional regulators that drive prostate cancer cell state transitions during disease 202 

progression, we screened key transcription factors (TFs) for their ability to promote 203 

three hallmark processes: neuroendocrine differentiation, castration resistance ( Fig. 204 

3a ), and progression toward higher Gleason grades. For these purposes, a list of 495 205 

human TFs ( Supplementary Table 3 ; See  Methods ) that overlapped with the Xenium 206 

gene panel was investigated. Then, in -silico overexpression for each TF was 207 

conducted and corresponding effects on the three processes were respectively 208 

evaluated to pinpoint candidate master regulators. 209 

Given that TFs often function at relatively low abundance levels, the previously 210 

used conventional perturbation metrics (endpoint score) may underestimate their 211 

effects. We thus developed a scoring strategy based on the area of cumulative 212 

perturbation curve (area score) ( Methods ) for more robust evaluation of perturbation 213 

effect. We first tested the utility of different scoring metrics on the screening of 214 

neuroendocrine differentiation. As expected, while the area -based score yielded 215 

results comparable to the endpoint scoring method for individual gene perturbation 216 

tasks ( Fig. S5 ), it proved particularly advantageous for batch TF screening, as 217 

evidenced by its improved accuracy in recognizing curated NE -associated TFs ( Figs. 218 

3b -d, Supplementary Table 4 ). Moreover, experimentally validated TFs  exhibited 219 

stronger perturbation effects and were more strongly enriched at the top of the 220 

prediction ranking compared to computationally inferred ones ( Figs. 3e -f, S6 ) using 221 

the score strategy. Consistently, top ranked TFs were significantly associated with 222 

neuron -related and neurodevelopmental pathways (Fig. 3g ). Similarly, key TFs 223 

identified by TMEformer in driving CRPC progression were significantly enriched 224 

among genes highly expressed in CRPC relative to other TFs  (Fig. 3h ). In addition, 225 

ranked enrichment analysis showed that CRPC -associated TFs were prominently 226 

enriched, particularly those related to the AR subtype ( Fig. 3i ), consistent with the 227 

elevated expression scores observed for AR -subtype TFs in the CRPC sample within 228 

our cohort ( Fig. 3j ). 229 

In parallel, we also screened for drivers that could facilitate PCa progression 230 

toward higher Gleason grades 25, 26 . Leveraging the treat -naï ve sample P020 with 231 

spatially well -defined Gleason 3 (G3) and Gleason 4 (G4) regions ( Fig. S7a ), we 232

4. TMEformer Enables Systemic in silico Perturbation of the TME factors 247 

Beyond intrinsic programs, we next leveraged the TMEformer perturbation framework 248 

to dissect how extrinsic TME alterations influence prostate cancer cell states by 249 

performing systematic ISPs on TME composition and ligands ( Fig. 4a ). Focusing on 250 

the NEPC phenotype, compositional perturbations revealed that myeloid cells exerted 251 

the most pronounced effect on NE markers compared with other TME cell types ( Figs. 252 

4b -c). However, two samples showed minimal response and were thus stratified into 253 

the low -response (Low -Resp) group, with the remaining samples classified as the high -254 

response (High -Resp) group ( Fig. 4d ). Despite comparable expression levels of 255 

canonical myeloid markers such as CD68, CD14 and CSF3R, the High -Resp group 256 

displayed higher expression of MRC1 and STAT6, markers associated with 257 

immunosuppressive phenotypes, whereas the Low -Resp group was linked to pro -258 

inflammatory signaling, including interferon -γ and TNF pathways ( Figs. S8a -c, 4e ). 259

associated with NE progression, while those in the Low -Resp group showed the 261 

opposite trend (Figs. 4f, S8d ). Correlation -ranked genes revealed significant 262 

enrichment of neutrophil -associated signatures, highlighting a potential contribution of 263 

N2 -like neutrophils to the observed perturbation responses ( Figs. 4g, S5e ). 264 

Interestingly, MRC1 expression displayed positive correlation in both groups ( Fig. 265 

4h ) and in -silico overexpression further demonstrated that MRC1 in myeloid cells 266 

promotes NE -like features in PCa cells ( Fig. 4i ). Analysis of scRNA -seq from mouse 267

6. Generalization of TME -aware Modeling to Different Sequencing Platforms and 353 

Cancer Types 354 

Finally, we investigated the generalizability of TMEformer across different spatial 355 

transcriptomic technologies and cancer types. Tumor stemness represents a critical 356 

determinant of therapeutic resistance and metastatic dissemination across diverse 357 

cancer types 48  and was therefore used as a benchmark. To this end, CD44, a well -358 

established marker of tumor stemness  49, 50 , was used as a molecular readout. We fine -359 

tuned TMEformer to predict CD44 expression in PCa cells and subsequently examined 360 

the perturbation effects of classical stemness -associated transcription factors  51 ,361 

namely OSKM (OCT4/POU5F1, SOX2, KLF4, and MYC). In silico upregulation of 362 

OSKM consistently led to a more pronounced increase in CD44 expression compared 363 

to other genes, both in our in -house dataset and in external PCa spatial transcriptomics 364 

datasets from Xenium and MERFISH platforms ( Fig. S13a ). We next extended this 365 

analysis to additional cancer types and found TMEformer also maintained robust 366 

predictive performance for CD44 response upon OSKM perturbation in both breast 367 

and ovarian cancer datasets (Fig. S13a ), suggesting that the model captures 368 

conserved regulatory mechanisms governing tumor stemness across cancers. 369 

Importantly, across all evaluated datasets and conditions, TMEformer generally 370 

outperformed baseline models ( Fig. S13b ), further highlighting the effectiveness of 371 

incorporating TME context into the modeling of gene regulatory responses. Moreover, 372 

we simulated the expansion of distinct TME components to assess their impact on 373

# Methods 382 

PCa cohort collection and spatial transcriptomic profiling 383 

Tumor tissues from eight patients with prostate cancer (PCa) were collected and 384 

subjected to spatial transcriptomic profiling using the 10x Genomics Xenium platform. 385 

The cohort comprised four treatment -naï ve (TN) tumors, three tumors obtained 386 

following androgen -deprivation therapy (ADT), and one castration -resistant prostate 387 

cancer (CRPC) sample. For the four TN samples, Gleason pattern –annotated regions 388 

were provided based on clinical pathological assessment. All tissue sections were 389 

profiled using the Xenium Prime 5K Human Panel. To enhance the detection of 390 

pathways associated with prostate cancer, we additionally designed a custom panel 391 

consisting of 51 PCa -relevant genes, resulting in a total of 5,051 genes in the standard 392 

assay. Raw Xenium output files were processed using the official Xenium analysis 393 

pipeline provided by 10x Genomics. Quality control (QC) filtering was applied to 394 

remove low -quality cells with fewer than 100 detected genes, as well as genes 395 

expressed in fewer than three cells. After QC, high -quality cells were retained for 396 

downstream expression normalization and dimensionality reduction using the Scanpy 397 

framework. Unsupervised clustering was performed using the Leiden algorithm, and 398 

major cell populations were annotated based on the expression of canonical marker 399 

genes referring to published literature 54 . For each cell during model training and 400 

prediction, genes were ranked based on their expression levels in descending order, 401 

and the resulting gene rankings were converted into token sequences following the 402 

processing pipelines of Geneformer  55 .403

TMEformer training 428

(https://huggingface.co/ctheodoris/Geneformer ). The gf -12L -95M -i4096 model 430 

(hereafter referred to as GF_PR) was pretrained on large -scale human single -cell 431 

transcriptomic datasets excluding cancer or immortalized cell lines. The gf -12L -95M -432 

i4096_CLcancer model (GF_CL) was subsequently continual -trained on human 433 

cancer -related single -cell dataset, which included limited representation of prostate 434 

cancer. In this study, annotated PCa cells were used to perform continual pretraining 435 

of the GF_CL model using a masked gene prediction objective. This objective enabled 436 

the model to capture tumor -intrinsic gene –gene relationships as well as interactions 437

performed with a batch size of 6, with gradient accumulation over 4 steps to achieve a 439 

larger effective batch size. The model was trained for a single epoch with a maximum 440

cosine learning rate scheduler with linear warmup over the first 1% of training steps 442

In Silico Perturbation (ISP) implementation 452 

To systematically simulate perturbations at both tumor -intrinsic and 453 

microenvironmental levels, we implemented three in silico perturbation (ISP) strategies 454 

based on TMEformer. 455 

(1) Target -Rank perturbation 456 

This strategy perturbs tumor -intrinsic gene regulation by modifying the expression 457 

ranking of one or multiple target genes within one cell, following the scheme introduced 458 

in Geneformer. For each cell, genes are ordered by expression level to form an input 459 

token sequence. To simulate gene overexpression (OE) or knockdown (KD), target 460 

genes are reassigned to higher or lower ranks within the sequence, respectively. The 461 

resulting changes in the cell embedding (output of the transformer encoder) are 462 

interpreted as the transcriptional impact of the perturbation. 463 

(2) TME -Composition perturbation 464 

To capture microenvironmental influences, we perturbed the cellular composition 465 

of the local tumor microenvironment. For each tumor cell, the proportion of a specific 466 

TME cell type among its neighboring cells was increased or decreased by resampling 467 

cells of the target type, while maintaining a fixed neighborhood size (k). The resulting 468 

changes in the final cell embedding were used to assess the impact of TME 469 

compositional shifts on tumor cells. 470 

(3) TME -Rank perturbation 471 

This strategy simulates gene -level perturbations within specific TME cell types. 472 

For OE perturbation, a two -step procedure was applied. First, within the selected TME 473 

cell type, non -expressing cells were replaced by cells expressing the target gene via 474 

resampling, thereby increasing the fraction of expressing cells. Second, for all cells of 475

ISP evaluation 481 

To quantitatively assess perturbation effects, we developed two complementary 482 

scoring schemes: an endpoint score and an area score. Unless otherwise specified, 483 

ISP evaluations reported in this study are based on the endpoint score. 484 

(1) Endpoint score 485 

The endpoint score measures the perturbation effect by placing the target gene at 486 

an extreme position in the rank -ordered gene sequence ( 𝐸 𝑒𝑛𝑑 ). Specifically, for each 487 

perturbation, the target gene is repositioned either to the first position, simulating gene 488 

overexpression (OE), or to the last position, simulating gene knockdown (KD). The 489 

resulting change relative to original cell ( 𝐸 𝑝𝑟𝑒 ) is defined as the endpoint perturbation 490 

effect. This scoring scheme is applicable across all ISP strategies, including Target -491 

Rank, TME -Composition, and TME -Rank perturbations. For TME -composition 492 

perturbations, the perturbation axis is defined by the relative fold change in the 493 

proportion of a selected TME cell type (e.g., a value of 2 indicates doubling its 494 

proportion, whereas 0.5 indicates reducing it by half). Perturbation -induced changes 495 

in prediction outputs are computed similarly to the gene -level ISP metrics. 496

Predictive tasks for ISP 515 

To explain the biological impact of in silico perturbation on PCa cells, we designed two 516 

predictive tasks: state embedding similarity analysis under a zero -shot setting and 517 

marker gene expression prediction via model fine -tuning. 518 

(1) State embedding similarity 519 

This task evaluates whether a given ISP drives a PCa cell from an initial cellular 520 

state (starting state) toward a predefined target state (end state). Under the zero -shot 521 

setting, up to 10,000 cells were randomly sampled from the end -state population to 522 

compute a mean reference embedding. For each of starting state samples, up to 1,000 523 

cells were randomly selected, and both their original (pre -ISP) and perturbed (post -524 

ISP) embeddings were obtained. Embeddings were extracted from the second -to -last 525 

transformer layer for embedding similarity analysis. Cosine similarity was then 526 

calculated between the end -state reference embedding and the starting -state 527 

embeddings before and after perturbation. The delta similarity score was defined as 528 

the difference between post - and pre -perturbation cosine similarity. A positive delta 529 

similarity (delta -sim) score indicates that the perturbation shifts the PCa cell 530 

representation closer to the end -state phenotype, whereas a negative score suggests 531 

divergence from the target state. 532

Background distribution and statistical testing 561 

To assess the statistical significance of in silico perturbation effects, background 562 

distributions were generated using randomly selected genes under the same 563 

perturbation settings as the target perturbations. For single -gene perturbations, 100 564 

genes were randomly sampled from the available panel genes. Each gene was 565 

perturbed in randomly selected cells, yielding 5,000 background perturbations for the 566 

embedding similarity task, with 50 cells perturbed per gene, and 10,000 background 567 

perturbations for the marker expression task, with 100 cells perturbed per gene. The 568 

resulting ISP scores were used to define the background distribution. For multi -gene 569 

perturbations involving  m genes, background distributions were generated by 570 

randomly sampling gene combinations of size  m and applying the same perturbation 571 

settings. 572 

To compare target perturbations with the background, each tissue section was 573 

divided into square patches of fixed size. Within each patch, the ISP score of each 574

within the same patch. Scores were then Z -score normalized within each patch 593 

separately for each marker gene. For each target perturbation, normalized scores were 594 

averaged across marker genes to obtain an overall delta -exp score. 595

Enrichment analysis 611

the python package gseapy with the  enrichr()  function. Pathway gene sets were 613

TME embedding -based clustering 662 

Based on the TME context encoding module of the TMEformer model, a TME context 663 

token embedding was computed for each PCa cell. These embeddings (termed TME -664 

emb) were used to perform unsupervised clustering, and the resulting TME -driven 665 

clusters were compared with clusters derived solely from the intrinsic transcriptomic 666 

profiles (termed EXP -emb) of PCa cells. The comparison focused on spatial coherence 667 

and pathological consistency. 668 

(1) TME Clustering Index 669 

To evaluate the spatial coherence of clusters, we quantified how spatially proximal 670 

cells within the same cluster were, compared with cells from different clusters. Two 671 

metrics were used: (i) Silhouette coefficient: Measures the separation between each 672 

cell’s assigned cluster and its nearest neighboring cluster. Higher values indicate more 673 

distinct and well -separated clusters in spatial space. (ii) iLISI_graph: Assesses the 674 

degree of cluster mixing by quantifying the presence of neighboring cells with different 675 

cluster labels. Lower mixing (i.e., lower iLISI_graph values) indicates higher spatial 676 

purity of clusters. Together, these two metrics form the TME clustering index 677

(2) Gleason Consistency Index 680 

To assess how well the TME -driven clusters correspond to pathological Gleason 681 

regions, we computed the Gleason consistency index based on two metrics: (i) 682 

Adjusted Rand Index (ARI): Measures the agreement between TME -based clusters 683 

and Gleason -labeled regions, adjusted for chance. Higher ARI indicates stronger 684 

alignment. (ii) Normalized Mutual Information (NMI): Quantifies shared information 685 

between clustering and pathology labels. Higher NMI reflects greater consistency in 686 

overall partition structure. Gleason Consistence Index from these metrics (ARI + NMI) 687 

jointly evaluate the degree to which TME -derived clustering captures clinically relevant 688 

pathological heterogeneity. The above original indexes (Silhouette, iLISI_graph, ARI, 689 

NMI) were computed using the scib python package 65 .690

TME embedding -based Gleason classification 692 

A unified Gleason classification pipeline was designed to evaluate the predictive 693 

capacity of the learned embedding from different models. In addition to TME -emb, 694 

other foundation model embeddings were extracted in a zero -shot manner. scVI was 695 

trained with n_latent = 512, n_layers = 2, and dropout_rate = 0.1. PCA was applied 696 

after standardization to reduce expression profiles to 512 dimensions using sklearn 697 

toolkit. Nicheformer and CellPLM embeddings were extracted using the corresponding 698 

default pipelines based on the latest model checkpoints. STACK embeddings were 699 

generated using the pretrained bc_large.ckpt model and reduced from 1600 to 512 700 

dimensions via PCA (97.7% variance retained). Random features were sampled from 701 

a standard normal distribution. All classifiers shared the same multilayer perceptron 702 

architecture (hidden layers: 512 –256 –128, ReLU, dropout = 0.3). The output layer had 703 

two units corresponding to binary Gleason region labels, optimized using the cross -704 

entropy loss. The classifier was trained using the Adam optimizer (learning rate = 0.001, 705 

batch size = 128) for three epochs in a cross -sample evaluation setting, where one 706 

sample was used for training and the remaining samples were used for evaluation. 707 

Performance was assessed using balanced accuracy, Matthews correlation coefficient 708 

(MCC), area under the ROC curve (AUC), and precision –recall AUC (PR AUC). For 709 

balanced accuracy and MCC, a fixed classification threshold of 0.5 was applied. 710

# Discussion 729 

In this study, we present TMEformer, a tumor microenvironment -aware transformer 730 

framework that models tumor cells as components of spatially organized cellular 731 

ecosystems. By integrating tumor -intrinsic programs with microenvironmental signals 732 

from clinical specimens which capture a rich set of endogenous perturbations such as 733 

disease states and tissue niches, TMEformer enables spatially informed in silico 734 

perturbation. This approach addresses a central challenge in tumor biology of 735 

understanding how perturbations propagate through spatially structured networks to 736 

shape cellular states. 737 

Across diverse perturbation settings, TMEformer outperformed Geneformer -738 

derived baselines, particularly in contexts where tumor evolution is tightly coupled to 739 

microenvironmental regulation. Notably, the model captures both tumor -intrinsic and 740 

non -cell -autonomous effects, allowing perturbations originating from neighboring cells 741 

(e.g. ligand signaling or compositional changes) to propagate and reshape tumor cell 742 

states. This provides the approach to dissect multi -level regulatory dependencies 743 

underlying neuroendocrine differentiation, castration resistance, and tumor 744

prioritizes clinically relevant candidates, including TWIST1, whose association with 746

their local ecological context. This leads to improved spatial coherence and stronger 752 

alignment with pathological architecture, indicating that spatial context provides 753 

biologically meaningful information not captured by transcriptome -only models. 754 

Notably, the generalization of TMEformer across spatial platforms and cancer types 755 

suggests that it captures conserved regulatory principles governing tumor –TME 756 

interactions. 757 

Despite these advances, several limitations remain. The reliance on predefined 758 

spatial neighborhoods and fixed weighting may limit the modeling of long -range or 759 

dynamic interactions. In addition, while in silico perturbation enables scalable 760 

hypothesis generation, experimental validation is required for newly identified 761 

regulators. The current framework is also limited to transcriptomic features and does 762 

not yet integrate other spatial modalities such as proteomics or chromatin accessibility. 763 

Future work may incorporate adaptive neighborhood modeling, multimodal spatial data 764 

integration, and temporally informed frameworks to better capture tumor evolution 765 

dynamics. Together, our findings support a shift from cell -centric modeling toward 766 

ecosystem -level representations of disease, where cellular behavior emerges from 767 

spatially structured interactions. By enabling predictive and perturbation -driven 768 

modeling of tumor ecosystems, TMEformer provides a foundation for mechanistic 769 

discovery and therapeutic prioritization in cancer. 770
