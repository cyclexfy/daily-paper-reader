Abstract 9

Background: Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) exhibits substantial molecular 10 

heterogeneity and lacks targeted receptor therapies. Single-omic approaches inadequately 11 

capture its regulatory complexity, necessitating integrated multi-omic frameworks to identify 12 

stable prognostic signatures. 13 

Methods: Matched transcriptomic and DNA methylation data from the TCGA-BRCA cohort 14 

were normalised and mathematically integrated to isolate disease-associated variations. A 15 

calibrated machine learning voting ensemble (comprising LightGBM, Random Forest, and 16 

Logistic Regression) was trained to predict clinical survival. Model generalisability was 17 

tested on an independent microarray cohort (GSE58812) using independent quantile 18 

normalisation. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values provided biological 19 

interpretability. 20 

Results: Differential and integrative analyses identified a 47-gene master prognostic 21 

signature. The ensemble classifier achieved an external validation accuracy of 74.77% (AUC 22 

0.590) on unseen clinical patients. SHAP analysis confirmed the biological directionality of 23 

these specific biomarkers in driving mortality. Hypergeometric pathway enrichment 24 

highlighted targetable metabolic and signalling networks. 25 

Conclusions: This multi-omic machine learning pipeline identifies a highly prognostic 47-26 

gene signature for TNBC. The model demonstrates strong cross-platform generalisability and 27 

offers interpretable clinical utility for stratifying patient risk and guiding future therapeutic 28 

target development. 29

Introduction 32 

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) represents an aggressive histological subgroup lacking 33 

oestrogen and progesterone receptor expression, as well as HER2 gene amplification [1]. 34 

Because these canonical targets are absent, systemic cytotoxic chemotherapy remains the 35 

primary intervention [2]. While initial response rates can be favourable, TNBC exhibits 36 

substantial intertumoural heterogeneity [3]. This leads to high frequencies of early relapse 37 

and poor overall survival rates [4]. 38 

Historically, molecular subtyping and prognostic biomarker discovery in TNBC have relied 39 

heavily on single-platform transcriptomic profiling [5]. While informative, these single-omic 40 

approaches inadequately capture the complex regulatory networks governing tumour 41 

progression [6]. DNA methylation, a primary epigenetic mechanism, frequently alters gene 42 

expression profiles in breast carcinomas through promoter silencing [7]. Analysing RNA 43 

expression in isolation ignores the upstream epigenetic modifications that dictate these 44 

transcriptomic states [8]. This often results in biomarker signatures with poor cross-cohort 45 

reproducibility [9]. To address this translational gap, integrative multi-omic frameworks are 46 

strictly required [10]. 47 

In this study, we present an integrated transcriptomic and epigenomic analysis of the TCGA-48 

BRCA TNBC cohort [11]. By mathematically fusing RNA-sequencing and DNA methylation 49 

arrays, we aim to isolate a stable, disease-associated molecular signature [12]. We 50 

subsequently train a calibrated machine learning ensemble to evaluate the prognostic utility 51 

of these selected features [13]. To ensure clinical applicability and mitigate cross-platform 52 

biases, the predictive model is validated on an independent, microarray-based external cohort 53 

(GSE58812) [14]. Finally, we utilise SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to 54 

provide biological interpretability [15]. This includes hypergeometric pathway enrichment 55 

identifying underlying biological pathways with potential therapeutic relevance [16]. 56 

Materials and Methods 57 

2.1. Cohort Selection and Data Acquisition 58 

To characterise the molecular architecture of TNBC, multi-omic datasets were acquired from 59 

public repositories. The primary discovery cohort was retrieved from The Cancer Genome 60

Atlas Breast Invasive Carcinoma (TCGA-BRCA) project [1]. Clinical metadata and 61 

biospecimen files were strictly filtered to isolate the TNBC phenotype, defined 62 

immunohistochemically by the lack of oestrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and human 63 

epidermal growth factor receptor 2 amplification [2]. Raw RNA-sequencing counts and 64 

matched DNA methylation beta-values were extracted for these patients. For external clinical 65 

validation, an independent microarray-based transcriptomic dataset (GSE58812) with 66 

matched survival data was retrieved from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) [5]. 67 

2.2. Multi-Omic Pre-processing and Differential Analysis 68 

All data pre-processing and statistical integration steps were conducted within the R 69 

programming environment. Raw RNA-sequencing counts are inherently subject to 70 

heteroscedasticity. To correct this library size bias, a variance stabilising transformation 71 

(VST) was applied to the TCGA-BRCA transcriptomic data prior to downstream analysis [7]. 72 

Epigenomic arrays were filtered to remove CpG probes with missing values greater than 73 

20%, and the remaining beta-values were harmonised to the transcriptomic matrix. 74 

Differential gene expression was calculated using the DESeq2 package (v1.42.0) [9], while 75 

differentially methylated regions (DMRs) were identified using the limma statistical 76 

framework (v3.58.0) [8]. Statistical significance across both biological modalities was strictly 77 

defined using Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-values ($padj < 0.05$) to control the false 78 

discovery rate. 79 

2.3. Integrative Feature Selection and Correlation Mapping 80 

Because single-omic profiles frequently fail to capture complex regulatory networks, the 81 

transcriptomic and epigenomic layers were mathematically integrated using Multi-Omics 82 

Factor Analysis (MOFA2 v1.4.0) [6]. Pairwise correlation analysis was performed to map the 83 

functional relationships between epigenetic promoter hypermethylation and corresponding 84 

RNA downregulation. By applying variance thresholding and multi-omic integration, the 85 

initial high-dimensional matrix was collapsed to a core consensus signature comprising 47 86 

core prognostic biomarkers. 87 

2.4. Machine Learning Ensemble Architecture 88 

The predictive modelling and explainable AI pipelines were deployed in a Python 89 

environment. A supervised machine learning pipeline was constructed, utilising the log2 fold-90 

change (log2FC) as the target variable for training, to predict patient survival outcomes based 91

2.5. External Validation and Cross-Platform Normalisation 100 

Evaluating computational models across heterogeneous sequencing platforms introduces 101 

severe technical biases. To robustly validate the TCGA-trained ensemble on the independent 102 

GSE58812 microarray cohort, independent quantile normalisation was applied to force 103 

identical statistical distributions between the training and testing matrices [7]. Algorithmic 104 

overconfidence inherent to tree-based base learners was corrected using Platt scaling 105 

(probability calibration) [16]. The optimal classification threshold for survival prediction was 106 

determined via Youden’s J-statistic [17], ensuring the model's generalisability to unseen 107 

clinical populations. 108 

2.6. Explainable AI and Functional Enrichment 109 

To determine the feature importance and direction of effect within the machine learning 110 

models, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values were computed using the SHAP 111 

library (v0.44.0) [18]. This framework quantified the positive or negative contribution of 112 

each biomarker to the final survival prediction. Finally, hypergeometric functional 113 

enrichment analysis was conducted using the Enrichr platform [19]. The 47-gene signature 114 

was mapped against the Reactome 2024 [20], KEGG 2026 [21], and WikiPathways Human 115 

2024 [22] databases to identify targetable metabolic and signalling networks driving the 116 

TNBC phenotype. 117

3.4. External Clinical Validation 155 

To establish the clinical generalisability of the signature, the TCGA-trained ensemble was 156 

tested on an entirely independent, microarray-based patient cohort (GSE58812). Full clinical 157 

and demographic metadata for this external validation cohort are provided in Supplementary 158 

Table 5. Following independent quantile normalisation to mitigate severe cross-platform 159 

batch effects, the model evaluated unseen patients, achieving an external validation accuracy 160 

of 74.77% (AUC 0.590). An ROC curve and a Confusion matrix were generated to 161 

demonstrate the prognostic classification accuracy of the TCGA-trained voting ensemble 162 

when applied to this external cohort (Figure 7A and Figure 7B). 163 

3.5. Explainable AI and Survival Stratification 164 

SHAP values were calculated to determine the contribution of individual features to the 165 

model's predictions. The SHAP summary plot provided explicit biological directionality, 166 

demonstrating how the relative upregulation or downregulation of specific genes within the 167 

47-biomarker panel directly pushed the model toward a patient mortality prediction (Figure 168 

6). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated patient stratification according to these 169 

molecular profiles, confirming the signature's capacity to accurately identify high-risk clinical 170 

trajectories over time (Figure 8). 171

Discussion 178 

The clinical management of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains challenged by 179 

severe molecular heterogeneity and the absence of canonical receptor targets. In this study, 180 

we engineered an integrated multi-omic machine learning pipeline to identify a stable, 181 

prognostic 47-gene signature. By mathematically fusing transcriptomic and epigenomic 182 

profiles, our ensemble classifier achieved a 74.77% external validation accuracy on an 183 

independent microarray cohort. This performance confirms that combining DNA methylation 184 

data with RNA sequencing yields superior prognostic stratification compared to single-omic 185 

methodologies. 186 

The computational model's predictions align with the known biological functions of the 187 

selected biomarkers. Features such as MEIS1, BMP4, and WWP2 emerged as critical 188 

prognostic nodes within our integrated matrix. Genomic analyses establish that epigenetic 189 

silencing of transcription factors actively drives aggressive oncogenic phenotypes. Our SHAP 190 

interpretability and functional enrichment mappings connect these specific transcriptomic 191 

alterations directly to dysregulated cell cycle control and metabolic signalling networks. This 192 

biological grounding indicates that the 47-gene signature does not merely correlate with 193 

patient survival mathematically, but tracks the mechanistic drivers of tumour progression. 194 

A primary translational barrier for genomic signatures is the technical bias introduced by 195 

varying sequencing platforms. We addressed this limitation by enforcing independent 196 

quantile normalisation between the TCGA discovery RNA-sequencing data and the 197 

GSE58812 validation microarray data. The stability of the classification metrics across these 198 

disparate platforms demonstrates high cross-cohort generalisability. Deploying a calibrated 199 

voting ensemble, incorporating LightGBM and Random Forest algorithms, allowed the 200 

capture of complex, non-linear gene interactions that traditional linear regression frameworks 201 

fail to detect. 202 

Despite these predictive advantages, specific limitations must be acknowledged. This study 203 

relies on retrospective data from public repositories, which may contain unmeasured clinical 204 

confounders. Bulk tumour sequencing inherently masks the contributions of the tumour 205 

microenvironment and distinct immune cell infiltration patterns. Future investigations should 206
