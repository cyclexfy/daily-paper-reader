# UHR-Net: An Uncertainty-Aware Hypergraph Refinement Network for Medical Image Segmentation 

Shuokun Cheng 1, Jinghao Shi 1, and Kun Sun 1

Abstract — Accurate lesion segmentation is crucial for clin-ical diagnosis and treatment planning. However, lesions often resemble surrounding tissues and exhibit ill-defined boundaries, leading to unstable predictions in boundary/transition regions. Moreover, small-lesion cues can be diluted by multi-scale feature extraction, causing under- or over-segmentation. To address these challenges, we propose an Uncertainty-Aware Hypergraph Refinement Network (UHR-Net). First, we in-troduce an Uncertainty-Oriented Instance Contrastive (UO-IC) pretraining strategy that couples geometry-aware copy-paste augmentation with hard-negative mining of lesion-like background regions to improve instance-level discrimination for small and visually ambiguous lesions. Second, we de-sign an Uncertainty-Guided Hypergraph Refinement (UGHR) block, which derives an entropy-based uncertainty map from a coarse probability map to guide hypergraph refinement. By splitting hyperedge prototypes into foreground and back-ground groups, UGHR decouples higher-order interactions and improves refinement in ambiguous regions. Experiments on five public benchmarks demonstrate consistent gains over strong baselines. Code is available at: https://github. com/CUGfreshman/UHR-Net .

Index Terms — Medical image segmentation, Uncertainty-Aware Hypergraph Refinement, Uncertainty-Oriented Instance Contrastive pretraining 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Medical image segmentation is a fundamental task in med-ical image analysis, providing critical structured information and quantitative evidence for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and therapeutic outcome assessment. In lesion seg-mentation tasks, automatically and reliably delineating lesion regions and their boundaries is particularly important for downstream quantitative measurements and decision support. Recent deep learning-based approaches have substantially improved segmentation performance. Nevertheless, in real-world clinical images, lesion segmentation accuracy is still hindered by two types of uncertainty. On the one hand, for instance-level discrimination and localization, small lesions occupy only a tiny fraction of pixels, and their discriminative cues are easily diluted during multi-scale feature extraction, leading to missed detections and inaccurate delineations. On the other hand, for pixel-level fine-grained prediction, lesions often resemble surrounding tissues and exhibit gradually transitioning boundaries. As a result, ambiguous regions lack clear separation signals and stable discriminative features, making models more prone to confusion in these areas. To tackle these challenges, existing studies have explored multiple directions. Convolutional neural network (CNN)-

They improve adapt-ability to complex morphologies and low-contrast lesions by refining multi-scale aggregation or introducing more flexible prediction heads. However, in many CNN segmentation approaches, uncertainty is often leveraged only indirectly, for example through loss re-weighting, regularization terms, or hard-sample mining, rather than being explicitly trans-formed into a guiding signal for subsequent structured re-finement or inference. To alleviate the limitations of local modeling, Transformer-based methods like H2Former [3] use self-attention to capture long-range dependencies and strengthen global interactions, while graph neural network (GNN) methods like ViG-UNet [4] construct sparse graph structures and employ message passing for more efficient global dependency modeling. Nevertheless, the core inter-actions in both families are still dominated by token-token or node-node pairwise relationships, which makes it difficult to explicitly model many-to-many higher-order consistency jointly determined by multiple regions. In this paper, we propose UHR-Net, an Uncertainty-Aware Hypergraph Refinement Network for lesion segmentation. To address cue dilution in small lesions and spurious foreground responses induced by lesion-like backgrounds, we introduce Uncertainty-Oriented Instance Contrastive (UO-IC) pretrain-ing to initialize the backbone. UO-IC uses geometry-aware copy-paste to generate images containing an original lesion instance and its downscaled replica, forming instance-level positive pairs, while mining lesion-like backgrounds as hard negatives. Contrastive learning among the original instance, the scaled instance, and the background strengthens instance-level discriminative representations. To improve prediction consistency in ambiguous regions, we further design an Uncertainty-Guided Hypergraph Refinement (UGHR) block. It derives an entropy-based uncertainty map from a coarse segmentation probability map to guide hypergraph refine-ment, strengthening context aggregation in ambiguous re-gions and enforcing many-to-many higher-order consistency. Moreover, foreground- and background-conditioned hyper-edge prototypes decouple higher-order aggregation path-ways, reducing boundary interference. Extensive experiments on ISIC-2016, ISIC-2017, GlaS, Kvasir-SEG, and Kvasir-Sessile demonstrate consistent gains and competitive perfor-mance. A. Uncertainty-Aware Medical Image Segmentation 

Medical image segmentation aims to delineate anatomical structures or lesions from images and serves as a funda-mental step for clinical diagnosis and treatment planning. Because lesions often resemble surrounding tissues and exhibit gradual transition boundaries, uncertainty modeling has become an important research direction. Early Bayesian deep learning studies distinguished aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty and showed that uncertainty estimates can im-prove robustness in dense prediction [5]. In medical imaging, later works further explored voxel-wise and lesion-wise uncertainty estimation for lesion detection and segmenta-tion [6]. More recently, uncertainty has been incorporated into the segmentation pipeline itself. UG-Net learns a coarse prediction and an uncertainty map in an end-to-end manner to guide feature refinement [7], while UACANet leverages uncertain regions to enhance context attention for polyp segmentation [8]. These studies indicate that uncertainty can help the model focus on ambiguous regions and improve prediction stability. However, most existing methods exploit uncertainty through confidence estimation, loss attenuation, or local attention, rather than transforming it into a structured cue for higher-order region interaction. They also seldom address, in a unified manner, both the dilution of small-lesion cues and the interference caused by lesion-like backgrounds. This limitation motivates us to further combine uncertainty guidance with structured relational modeling. 

B. Hypergraph Learning 

Beyond exploiting uncertainty to characterize ambiguous regions, another related line of research focuses on struc-tured relational modeling. In recent years, several segmen-tation methods have introduced graph-based operators to better capture structured long-range dependencies that are not fully modeled by convolution or self-attention [4]. Yet conventional graphs are still built on pairwise edges, which are often insufficient for dense prediction, where multiple pixels or patches may jointly correspond to one lesion structure or boundary transition. Hypergraph learning offers a more expressive alternative by allowing each hyperedge to associate a group of semantically related vertices and enabling higher-order message passing. Early studies such as HGNN and HGNN+ established general hypergraph con-volution frameworks [9], [10]. Building on these advances, hypergraph modeling has gradually been explored in visual recognition and medical image analysis, showing promise for aggregating higher-order context across spatially sepa-rated but semantically correlated regions [11]. Nevertheless, current hypergraph-based segmentation methods mainly fo-cus on generic feature aggregation or topology construc-tion, and rarely use uncertainty cues to determine where higher-order reasoning should be strengthened. In contrast, our method uses an entropy-derived uncertainty prior to guide node-hyperedge interaction and employs decoupled foreground/background prototype groups, making refinement more targeted in ambiguous lesion regions. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

A. Overview 

To alleviate small-lesion cue dilution, lesion-like background-induced spurious foreground responses, and unstable predictions in ambiguous regions, we propose UHR-Net for medical lesion segmentation. The core idea is to couple instance-level discriminative representation enhancement with structured refinement for ambiguous regions: during pretraining, we guide the backbone to learn finer-grained and more discriminative cues; during end-to-end training, we introduce a coarse segmentation probability map and an uncertainty-guided refinement mechanism implemented by multi-scale UGHR blocks, so that refinement focuses more on ambiguous regions, thereby improving prediction stability. As shown in Fig. 1, given an input image I, UHR-Net outputs the final foreground probability map ˆY , with the cor-responding ground truth denoted as Y . The training pipeline consists of two parts: UO-IC pretraining and end-to-end training. In UO-IC, geometry-aware copy-paste constructs positive pairs and mines lesion-like hard negatives under an InfoNCE objective. In end-to-end training, a lightweight guidance head produces a coarse segmentation probability map, denoted as ˆM . An entropy-based uncertainty map, denoted as U , is then derived from ˆM to guide multi-scale UGHR blocks. In each UGHR block, the UGHR branch builds foreground- and background-conditioned hyperedge prototypes and modulates node-hyperedge participation log-its with U , while a parallel convolutional branch provides local enhancement. Finally, the decoder aggregates refined features and outputs ˆY . Details are provided in Section III-C.

C. Uncertainty-Guided Hypergraph Refinement block 

Along the refinement path, the UGHR block at the i-th scale takes a fused input feature ˜di, which is obtained by combining the channel-aligned feature at the current scale with cross-scale information from the deeper refined output. Meanwhile, the coarse segmentation probability map 

ˆM is resized to this scale to obtain ˆMi. Given ˜di and 

ˆMi, the UGHR block outputs the refined feature ei. Since ambiguous regions typically have higher uncertainty and larger prediction fluctuations, the UGHR branch derives a pixel-wise uncertainty map Ui from ˆMi and uses it to modulate the node-hyperedge participation logits before nor-malization. This modulation amplifies the participation logits at highly uncertain locations, encouraging larger normalized participation weights after normalization, so that subsequent hypergraph message passing can more effectively aggregate contextual information from these regions, improving predic-tion consistency in ambiguous regions. Next, we introduce uncertainty-guided hypergraph construction (Section III-C.1) and hypergraph message passing and feature updating (Sec-tion III-C.2) in turn. 

1) Uncertainty-Guided Hypergraph Construction: We compute a pixel-wise uncertainty map Ui from the coarse segmentation probability map ˆMi:

Ui = − ˆMi log( ˆMi + ε) + (1 − ˆMi) log(1 − ˆMi + ε)log 2 , (4) where ε is a numerical stability constant, and log 2 nor-malizes the binary entropy to [0 , 1] . When ˆMi is close to 

0.5, Ui attains larger values, which typically correspond to ambiguous regions. In what follows, Ui is used to modulate the node-hyperedge participation logits, yielding normalized participation weights. Next, we treat each pixel location in ˜di as a node and introduce two groups of hyperedge prototypes, i.e., fore-ground and background, so that hyperedges can selectively aggregate node information over the entire image. Prototype generation is conditioned on the coarse segmentation prior: we use ˆMi and 1 − ˆMi as soft weights for the foreground and background, respectively, and adopt a lightweight Mask Context Extraction (MCE) module to perform mask-guided pooling on ˜di, producing a foreground context vector cFG 

and a background context vector cBG . Considering that coarse predictions in ambiguous regions can be mixed, we further apply bidirectional cross-attention between the two contexts to obtain ˜cFG and ˜cBG , which are used to generate sample-wise offsets and thus construct sample-wise dynamic prototypes. A. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate UHR-Net on five public datasets: Kvasir-SEG [26], Kvasir-Sessile [27], GlaS [28], ISIC-2016 [29], and ISIC-2017 [30] following the same split and setting as [21]. All experiments are implemented using PyTorch. For the UO-IC pretraining stage, we set τ = 0 .10 and λic = 1 .0.For the end-to-end training stage, we set λaux = 0 .1. In UGHR block, we set M = 8 prototypes per group and 

β = 1 .0. For both stages, we set the batch size to 16 and use the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10 −4.

B. Comparison with Other Methods 

Tables I and II summarize the quantitative comparisons with representative methods on five datasets. On Kvasir-Sessile, Kvasir-SEG, and GlaS, UHR-Net achieves the best mIoU of 83.5%, 85.0%, and 87.0%, and the best mDSC of 90.6%, 90.8%, and 92.7%, respectively. Specifically, on the GlaS dataset, UHR-Net improves mIoU by 1.9% over the second-best method. On ISIC-2016 and ISIC-2017, UHR-Net attains the best mIoU of 87.6% and 81.8% and achieves strong mDSC of 92.9% and 89.2%, respectively, showing clear gains over strong existing baselines. These results show that UHR-Net achieves consistently strong perfor-mance across the five benchmarks. Fig. 2 presents qualitative comparisons on ISIC-2016 and Kvasir-SEG, including U-Net++, ESPNet, CMUNeXt, ConDSeg, our method, and the ground truth.

To verify the proposed components and analyze the sen-sitivity to key hyperparameters, including the hyperedge prototype number M , the logit modulation strength β, the InfoNCE temperature τ , and the contrastive loss weight λic ,we conduct ablation studies and hyperparameter sensitivity experiments on the Kvasir-SEG dataset. 

Effectiveness of proposed components. We conduct ab-lation studies on the proposed components, with results summarized in Table III. Guid., and FG/BG HG, while Exp. 7 is the full model. In these experiments, when Base HR is turned off, we replace the UGHR branch in the UGHR block with an equivalent standard convolutional layer. When the Unc. Guid. is turned off, the entropy-based uncertainty map U is no longer used to modulate the node-hyperedge participation logits. Enabling Base HR (Exp. 3) brings a clear gain over Exp. 1, validating that hypergraph-based higher-order aggregation is beneficial for refining pre-dictions. On top of Base HR, FG/BG HG (Exp. 4) and Unc. Guid. (Exp. 5) each further improve performance: FG/BG HG reduces foreground-background interference by decou-pling their prototype groups, while Unc. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present UHR-Net, an Uncertainty-Aware Hypergraph Refinement Network for medical lesion segmen-tation that tackles cue dilution in small lesions and prediction instability in ambiguous regions. We introduce UO-IC pre-training to enhance instance discriminability via geometry-aware copy-paste and lesion-like hard-negative mining. We further develop UGHR blocks that (i) derive an entropy-based uncertainty map to modulate node-hyperedge partici-pation logits, and (ii) employ foreground- and background-conditioned hyperedge prototypes to decouple higher-order interactions. Extensive experiments on five public bench-marks demonstrate consistent gains over representative meth-ods. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
