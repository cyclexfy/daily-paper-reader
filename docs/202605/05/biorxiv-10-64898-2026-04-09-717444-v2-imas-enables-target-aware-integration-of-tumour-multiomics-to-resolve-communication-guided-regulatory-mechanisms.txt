Abstract 

Tumour multiomic datasets are often sparse, heterogeneous and limited in size, hindering robust and interpretable discovery of regulatory mechanisms. Here we present IMAS, a target-aware integrative framework for multiomic data augmentation and mechanism prioritization that leverages a pan-cancer single-cell multiomic resource to contextualize new tumour datasets and identify reliable sample-specific mechanistic hypotheses. IMAS combines shared latent-space modelling with target-domain adaptation to improve correspondence between predicted and observed RNA and TF profiles while concentrating explanatory predictive supports within the target dataset. Building on this adapted representation, IMAS reconstructs structured RNA–TF coupling networks, refines intercellular signaling through ligand-informed communication modelling, and organizes regulatory programs along communication-associated ordering. In independent colon cancer data, IMAS improved cluster-resolved correspondence and revealed communication-guided regulatory cascades across malignant epithelial states. A LAMB1-centred analysis further demonstrates how the framework supports progressive reinforcement of local regulatory structure and enables perturbation-based probing of context-specific dependencies. Rather than exhaustively predicting all possible outcomes, IMAS provides a target-aware and interpretable strategy to construct consistent and interpretable mechanism-discovery scaffolds and prioritize regulatory dependencies in data-limited tumour systems.

1. Introduction 

Single-cell multiomic profiling has made it increasingly feasible to interrogate tumour regulatory states at high resolution by jointly capturing transcriptional and chromatin-level information within the same cells 1-3 . In principle, these data enable the integration of gene expression 4,5 , transcription factor (TF) activity and regulatory-element usage into a unified cellular framework 6,7 , providing a direct route to reconstruct tumour regulatory programs beyond transcriptomic analysis alone 8.In practice, however, tumour multiomic datasets rarely support reliable mechanism discovery. These data are typically limited in sample size 9,10 , incompletely sample cellular states, and exhibit substantial heterogeneity across patients, datasets and tumour types 11-13 . These constraints are particularly pronounced in complex tumour microenvironments 14,15 , where regulatory signals must be inferred from sparse, noisy and highly context-dependent observations 16,17 . As a result, tumour multiomic data represent high-dimensional and only partially observed systems in which exhaustive recovery of regulatory mechanisms is not feasible. More importantly, even identifying a consistent subset of mechanistically meaningful dependencies remains challenging under realistic data-limited conditions. Recent approaches have attempted to address these limitations by leveraging large public atlases or cross-dataset training strategies to improve predictive performance in target datasets 18-20 . While these methods can recover shared structure across studies, their utility for mechanism discovery remains limited 21 . A key limitation is structural fragmentation: cross-modal prediction models focus on recovering missing

Consequently, intracellular regulatory coupling, intercellular signaling and perturbation responses are rarely integrated into a single, target-specific framework. This separation limits the ability to move from predictive modelling to a coherent and mechanistically interpretable view of tumour regulatory organization. Here we present IMAS, a prior-constrained, target-aware integrative multi-omic framework designed to prioritize mechanistically coherent regulatory dependencies in data-limited tumour settings. IMAS is built on a pan-cancer single-cell multiomic foundation that captures transferable regulatory structure across tumour contexts and refines this representation within a target dataset under explicit prior constraints. This design separates global representation learning from target-specific adaptation, enabling preservation of shared regulatory organization while concentrating target-relevant predictive support into a compact and structured set of dependencies. Within this adapted representation, IMAS reconstructs RNA–TF coupling networks and integrates ligand-informed intercellular communication constraints to link intracellular regulation with extracellular signaling. In addition, IMAS enables perturbation-oriented interrogation of local regulatory structure within the same modelling framework, allowing candidate dependencies to be systematically probed rather than inferred in isolation.

We applied IMAS to independent colon cancer datasets and found that target-aware adaptation improves correspondence between predicted and observed profiles while concentrating predictive support into features that are structurally aligned with cell-state organization. The adapted representation enables reconstruction of communication-constrained RNA–TF regulatory programs and supports perturbation-oriented prioritization of local dependencies within the target context. Importantly, we further evaluate these dependencies using external perturbation evidence, comparative benchmarking against existing methods, and quantitative assessment of communication-constrained organization. Together, these results position IMAS as a pan-cancer-to-target transfer framework that does not aim to exhaustively enumerate all possible tumour mechanisms, but instead provides a strategy for identifying the most consistent, interpretable and experimentally testable regulatory dependencies under realistic conditions of data limitation and heterogeneity.

3. Result 3.1 Construction of a pan-cancer multiomic resource and overall design of IMAS 

To establish a transferable basis for tumour multiomic analysis, we assembled a pan-cancer single-cell multiomic corpus comprising matched RNA and chromatin accessibility profiles across diverse cancer types and datasets. This resource was projected into unified RNA, TF and ATAC feature spaces and used to train the IMAS baseline model, which integrates cross-modal alignment with prior-supported regulatory structure 25,26 . Discussion 

Tumour single-cell multiomic datasets pose a fundamental challenge for mechanism discovery because they are sparse, heterogeneous and only partially observed across regulatory layers 71-73 . Under these conditions, reconstructing a complete causal regulatory system is not feasible from sequencing data alone 74 . Instead, a more tractable objective is to identify a subset of regulatory dependencies that are both internally consistent and biologically plausible within a given dataset 75,76 . In this study, we developed IMAS as a framework designed for this purpose: to prioritize mechanistically coherent regulatory dependencies in data-limited tumour settings by combining transferable multiomic structure with target-specific adaptation and

constraint. Importantly, this shifts the analytical objective from exhaustive network reconstruction toward identification of functionally constrained dependency structures that are both interpretable and experimentally tractable. A central conceptual advance of this work is that target-aware adaptation does not merely improve predictive correspondence, but reorganizes predictive support into a more compact and structured form that is closely aligned with cell-state organization. This effect suggests that transfer learning, when constrained by prior regulatory structure, can function as a mechanism-selection process rather than a simple domain-correction step. By concentrating predictive support into a smaller subset of features that are both structurally and functionally relevant, IMAS reduces the effective search space of regulatory hypotheses and increases the interpretability of model outputs in the target dataset. In this sense, IMAS reframes transfer learning as a structure-refinement process that selectively amplifies biologically meaningful regulatory signals. Beyond prediction, IMAS enables reconstruction of regulatory organization across multiple levels within a unified representation. In particular, the integration of RNA– TF coupling with ligand-informed communication constraints allows intracellular transcriptional programs to be interpreted in the context of intercellular signaling. Rather than treating communication as a downstream descriptive layer, this framework incorporates communication constraints directly into the regulatory structure, resulting in more selective and organized signaling–response relationships.
